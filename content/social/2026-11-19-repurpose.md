## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets)

1/ **Balance transfer cards sound like a cheat code**: 0% APR for 12-21 months, move your debt, pay it off interest-free. For some people, this saves thousands. For others? It deepens the debt trap. Here's what actually happens 🧵

2/ **The math looks good on paper**: Transfer $6k at 3% fee ($180) → owe $6,180 at 0% for 18 months → pay $343/month and you're done. Compare that to $6k at 22% APR ($2,650 in interest over 36 months). Net savings? ~$2,470.

3/ **But here's the trap issuers count on**: They collect the fee upfront (3-5%), make money on remaining balances after the promo ends, and bet you'll either overspend, miss the deadline, or rack up new charges. It's calculated business, not charity.

4/ **Trap #1: The fee eats your savings**. Transferring $1,500 with a 5% fee ($75) might only net you $65 in savings if the promo is short. Do the math before you apply—sometimes the juice isn't worth the squeeze.

5/ **Trap #2: You won't qualify for enough credit**. Have $25k in debt across maxed-out cards? Most issuers won't approve you for a line large enough to transfer it all. You'll move $7k and still owe $18k at high rates elsewhere.

6/ **Trap #3: New purchases destroy the plan**. That 0% only applies to transferred balances. New charges accrue interest immediately at 18-25% APR. And payment rules mean your payments go to the 0% first—new purchases just sit there bleeding money.

7/ **Trap #4: The promo ends and you're not done**. Still owe $6k when 18 months expires? It immediately hits 18-29% APR. You've just moved your debt in a circle, possibly made it *more* expensive. This is where most people get trapped.

8/ **Trap #5: You keep using the old cards**. Transfer $5k to a 0% card, then run up $3k in new charges on the old ones. Congrats, you now have $8k in debt instead of $5k. The transfer moved money around—it didn't fix the spending problem.

9/ **When balance transfers ACTUALLY work**:
✓ Concrete monthly payoff plan (do the math)
✓ Savings outweigh the fee
✓ You've fixed the spending habits that created the debt
✓ You treat it like a 0% loan, not a fresh start to spend again

10/ **The bottom line**: Balance transfers aren't magic. They're a tool that works *only* with discipline. If you can't commit to 18 months of solid payments and behavioral change, you're just kicking the can down the road. Know the traps. Do the math. Execute ruthlessly.
