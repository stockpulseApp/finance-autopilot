# Balance Transfer Cards Repurposed

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

1/ That 0% APR balance transfer card sounds like salvation. It's not. Not unless you know the five traps that turn breathing room into a deeper hole. Thread 🧵

2/ Here's the math nobody does: Transfer $9,785, make $200 minimum payments for 18 months = $6,185 still owed when 0% ends. Then 24.99% APR hits. You'll pay $3,837 MORE in interest than if you'd never transferred. 

3/ Trap #1: The intro period is NOT optional. You must eliminate the ENTIRE balance before 0% expires or you'll face revert rates often HIGHER than your original cards (sometimes 25%+). Reverse-engineer your payment first.

4/ Trap #2: Your old cards now show $0 and available credit. Psychologically, that feels like free money. People spend on them immediately. Suddenly you're carrying transferred debt + new debt. You haven't reduced anything—you've increased it.

5/ Trap #3: That 3-5% balance transfer fee? It's real money. Transfer $10,000 at 5% = $500 gone immediately. Only do this if your interest savings exceed the fee by at least $300. Do the math BEFORE applying.

6/ Trap #4: Approval ≠ your full debt amount. Applied hoping to move $18K? Got approved for $7.5K instead. Now you're juggling which balances to transfer while others stay at 22% APR. Most issuers cap limits at 30-40% of annual income.

7/ Trap #5: Serial transfers without fixing behavior. Transfer balance → make minimum payments → transfer again when 0% ends. You're just parking debt, not eliminating it. This cycle costs you years and thousands.

8/ The smart move: Calculate monthly payment needed to clear balance completely during intro period. If you can't afford it, a fixed-rate personal loan might force the discipline a credit card won't.

9/ Freeze your old cards after transfer. Don't spend a dollar on the new card beyond transferred balance. Treat it like a loan, not a credit card. This tool only works if you're committed to elimination, not just relief.

10/ Balance transfer cards are powerful. But only if you dodge the traps. Don't move debt—eliminate it. That's the difference between smart payoff and expensive procrastination.
