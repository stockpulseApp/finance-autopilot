# Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ Your brain is wired to avoid saber-toothed tigers, not market volatility. That's why it screams "WAIT" every time you try to invest during scary headlines. Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the loophole.

2/ Here's the math: Invest $1,500/month vs. $18,000 lump sum. When markets dip, your monthly buys MORE shares at cheaper prices. When they spike, it buys fewer. Over time? You win through boring consistency.

3/ Real example: Sarah invests $1,500/month for 6 months across a market that swings from $80-$105/share. She gets 98.15 shares at $91.71 average cost. Her colleague Tom waits, invests all $9K at $105. He gets 85.71 shares. Same money, fewer shares.

4/ The kicker: Tom's strategy wins 2 out of 3 times in pure bull markets. But here's what matters—DCA keeps you *actually investing* instead of paralyzed by FOMO and fear. Consistency beats perfection.

5/ Three scenarios where DCA saves you: (1) You're new and markets feel terrifying (2) You get income in chunks—bonuses, freelance work (3) You're one bad headline away from staying in cash forever

6/ Setup takes 5 minutes: Pick total amount + timeline → Set auto-transfers → Choose low-cost index fund → Ignore the noise for a year → Done. Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab all have this built in.

7/ The trap: Don't overthink it. Not daily checking. Not switching funds. Not trying to "add more on dips." Set it, forget it, rebalance once yearly. Your future self will thank you.

8/ DCA won't save you in a 50-year bear market (they don't exist). But it will save you from panic-selling at bottoms—and that costs most investors more than market timing ever could.
