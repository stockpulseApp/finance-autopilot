```
Consolidation works like this: borrow money at a lower rate, pay off high-interest debts, service one loan instead of many.

Simple in theory. But 72% of people who consolidate end up re-borrowing on their cleared credit cards within 18 months.

2/10
```

```
✅ CONSOLIDATION HELPS IF:

• You qualify for a rate meaningfully lower than your current debt (20%+ cards → 10% loan = real savings)
• You can commit to NOT reusing the cards
• You're drowning in missed payments due to tracking chaos
• Your new term matches your payoff timeline

3/10
```

```
❌ CONSOLIDATION BACKFIRES IF:

• You extend the loan to 72 months just to lower monthly payments (you pay MORE interest overall)
• Your credit score drops right before you apply for a mortgage/auto loan
• You don't fix the spending habits that created the debt

4/10
```

```
Real math: $20K debt at 21% APR pays off in 46 months. Consolidate into 72 months at 13%? You're paying $2,200 MORE in total interest.

The trap: "But my monthly payment dropped $200!" Yes. For 26 extra months.

5/10
```

```
The credit score hit is real but temporary:
• Hard inquiry: -5 to 10 points
• New account age lowers average: -10 to 20 points
• Closing old cards = higher utilization: -10 to 30 points

Most recover in 6–12 months. But if you're near a rate threshold? Timing matters.

6/10
```

```
The #1 consolidation killer: debt didn't accumulate from nowhere. If you consolidate without building an emergency fund, cutting unnecessary spending, or fixing cash flow — you'll accumulate new debt while servicing the old.

Consolidation treats symptoms, not the disease.

7/10
```

```
Not all "consolidation" is real. Debt settlement companies market as consolidators but actually:
• Stop your payments (destroys credit for years)
• Collect fees upfront
• Negotiate down debts you can't verify will actually be reduced

Know the difference.

8/10
```

```
💡 The consolidation decision tree:

1. Can you get a rate 4%+ lower than your current average? NO → don't consolidate
2. Will you close/freeze the paid-off cards? NO → consolidation fails
3. Does your new term match your payoff capacity? NO → you're extending pain

9/10
```

```
Bottom line: Consolidation is a *tool*, not a solution.

For disciplined borrowers with high-rate debt? It saves thousands.

For everyone else? It's trading one problem (high interest) for another (new debt + extended repayment).

Know yourself first.

10/10
```
