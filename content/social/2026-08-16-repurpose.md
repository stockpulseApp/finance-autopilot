## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ The "delay Social Security until 70" advice is bulletproof—until it isn't.

Yes, you get 77% more per month. But only if you live past 80. And only if your life doesn't look like most Americans'.

When early claiming actually wins 🧵

2/ The math: Claim at 62 ($1,400/mo) vs. 70 ($2,480/mo). By 80, the age-70 claimer breaks even. Die at 78? You left $20k+ on the table by waiting.

3/ Real talk: If your parents died in their 70s, or you're retiring with <$250k in savings, betting on longevity is a bad wager. Poor health changes everything.

4/ The savings problem: Retire at 64 with $220k. To delay SS until 70, you burn $180k from savings before your first check. You're left with $40k. One emergency and you're out.

5/ Sequence-of-returns risk is real. Market crashes in year 1. Portfolio down 25%. Now you're selling low for 6+ years while waiting. You might run out of money by 75.

6/ Married? One spouse claims early for income. The other delays to 70 to maximize the survivor benefit. That's optimization.

7/ Delaying DOES make sense if: You're healthy, family lived into 90s, have $600k+, or still working (claiming early = earnings penalties).

8/ The real decision framework:
- Health: Do you expect to see 80?
- Savings: Can you afford to wait?
- Risk: Can your portfolio absorb 8 years of withdrawals?

One formula doesn't fit all.

9/ Your financial planner's "delay until 70" isn't wrong. It's just incomplete. Run the break-even. Map your scenario. Then decide.

10/ Don't optimize for the textbook retirement. Optimize for YOUR life.
```
