## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ Emotional trades cost investors $2,237 on average. Your feelings about the stock market are expensive. Here's how to stop them from wrecking your portfolio.

2/ The problem: When markets tank, loss aversion kicks in. We feel losses twice as intensely as gains. So we sell low. Panic becomes expensive. 📉

3/ The solution: Dollar-cost averaging. Invest the same fixed amount on the same schedule—regardless of market chaos. Math, not emotion, drives the strategy.

4/ How it works: $500/month into an index fund. Market drops? Your $500 buys MORE shares. Market rises? Fewer shares. Over time, you're buying at lower average prices.

5/ The data: During 2022's brutal bear market, disciplined DCA investors accumulated shares at $355-360. By 2023, those same shares rebounded to $440+. Timing the market loses. Staying invested wins.

6/ Real example: Emily invested $500/month through 2022's crash (worst timing possible). By Dec 2023, she was up 17.4% on her 2022 investments alone. Discipline > panic.

7/ The psychology: Smaller regular investments feel less consequential than one big bet. Your brain stops screaming "sell!" and just executes the plan.

8/ DCA isn't perfect—lump sum investing usually beats it mathematically. But only if you actually *deploy* your cash. Most people freeze when it matters most.

9/ The real win: DCA forces you to buy when markets are terrifying. That's exactly when opportunities exist. Fear = opportunity for the disciplined.

10/ Your move: Set up automatic investments in a low-cost index fund. Ignore the headlines. Check back in 10 years. That's how wealth compounds.
