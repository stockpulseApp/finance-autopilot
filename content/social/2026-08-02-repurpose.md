# House Hacking Repurposed Content

## X Thread (10 Tweets)

1/ House hacking = live in part of your home, rent the rest. First-time buyers are using this to slash housing costs 40-70%. We ran the math. It's wild. 🧵

2/ Classic example: Maya bought a $485K duplex in DC. Lives in one unit, rents the other for $2,200/mo. Her effective housing cost? $840/month. Her tenant is paying down her principal. (Actual case study from 2025 buyers)

3/ Here's why it works: A $450K home with 5% down = $3,040/mo payment. Rent out half for $1,800? You're down to $1,240. Over 5 years, that rental income funds ~$50K of your loan balance.

4/ You don't need 20% down. FHA loans let you buy 2-4 unit properties with just 3.5% down if you occupy one unit. Credit score 580+? You're eligible. Even 500-579 scores work with 10% down.

5/ The financing hack: Lenders count 75% of projected rental income toward your debt-to-income ratio. That $1,600 rental income actually counts as $1,200 toward your qualifying income. It helps you borrow more.

6/ Four models: (1) Rent rooms in a house, (2) Live in one unit of a duplex/triplex, (3) Build an ADU, (4) Airbnb a bedroom. Each has different pros/cons depending on your privacy tolerance & market.

7/ BUT—this isn't free money. You're screening tenants, handling 2am maintenance calls, dealing with evictions, navigating landlord-tenant law. Bad tenant = thousands in losses. Screening is non-negotiable.

8/ Tax benefits most people sleep on: mortgage interest deduction, depreciation, repair write-offs, utilities, property management fees. Consult a CPA—the savings are significant.

9/ Real talk: House hacking works in high-rent metros & markets where rent significantly exceeds mortgage costs. In low-rent areas? The math doesn't pencil out. Run your local numbers first.

10/ Bottom line: House hacking isn't a get-rich-quick scheme. It's a wealth accelerator for disciplined first-time buyers willing to share walls for 3-5 years and build equity while their tenants pay their mortgage.
