## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ Markets hit record highs. Then they wobble. You freeze.

You tell yourself you'll invest "when things settle down."

Here's the problem: that moment never arrives. And the cost of waiting is higher than you think.

The antidote? Dollar-cost averaging. A boring strategy that works *with* human psychology.

2/ Dollar-cost averaging = investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of market conditions.

$1,000/month for 12 months beats trying to time the "perfect" entry point.

You're not trying to outsmart the market. You're automating it.

3/ Why your brain hates investing during volatility:

Loss aversion. We feel pain from losses 2x more intensely than pleasure from gains.

A $1,000 drop before a recovery hits *hard* emotionally.

DCA spreads that pain across smaller purchases over time.

4/ The data: lump-sum investing wins ~68% of the time.

But here's the twist—that assumes you actually *stay invested*.

If you panic-sell after a dip, you lock in losses forever.

DCA keeps you in the game when emotions run high.

5/ Real talk: DCA only works if you execute it.

Fear of investing all $15K today? Spread it over 12 months.

The small performance drag is worth the peace of mind that keeps you from bailing.

6/ When DCA makes sense:
- Lump sum + genuine fear of deploying it all
- Elevated volatility or after sharp run-ups
- New investors building the habit
- Already doing it via 401(k) contributions

When to skip it:
- High risk tolerance + long horizon
- Trying to time the market
- Uninvested cash earning 0%

7/ The real win with DCA isn't perfect timing.

It's psychological. It's staying invested through fear.

History shows: investors who sold low and stayed on sidelines permanently damaged returns.

Staying in beats timing perfectly.
```
