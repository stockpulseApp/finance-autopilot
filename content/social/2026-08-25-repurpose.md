## X Thread (10 tweets)

1/ **The $200K question nobody asks until it's too late:** When do you claim Social Security? Choose 62 vs. 70, and you're looking at a 30% permanent benefit cut—or an 8% annual raise. The math is stark. The "right" answer? It depends on YOU. 🧵

2/ Real numbers: Someone with a $2,000 monthly PIA at 67 gets $1,400/month at 62 (lifetime: $386K), but $2,480/month at 70 (lifetime: $446K). Break-even? Around age 80-82. The question: Will YOU live past that?

3/ Dave Ramsey's hot take: Claim at 62 if you have assets and will *invest* every check. The logic: 5 extra years of compounding beats one bigger check later. But this only works if you have a financial cushion AND investor discipline. Most people don't have both.

4/ Here's the real risk: You think you'll die young (your dad did at 76), so you claim early. Then you live to 89 like your mom. Now you're collecting 30% less every month—forever. Longevity insurance works both ways.

5/ For married couples, it's even trickier. When one spouse dies, the survivor gets the *higher* benefit—but loses the smaller one. This makes delaying for the high earner strategically powerful. Your spouse's longevity matters as much as yours.

6/ The median American claims at 66 (full retirement age)—a middle-of-the-road compromise. But median isn't optimal for *you*. Your health, assets, spouse's earnings, and risk tolerance all shift the calculus.

7/ Medicare complication: Claim Social Security at 62, and you auto-enroll in Medicare Parts A & B at 65. That can create conflicts with employer coverage. It's a small detail with big coordination costs. Read the fine print.

8/ The math-optimal strategy for many: Higher earner delays to 70 (locks in survivor protection + 8% annual raises). Lower earner claims earlier (liquidity + household income smoothing). But this assumes you'll stay together—and stay disciplined.

9/ Before you decide: Calculate your break-even age. Use SSA.gov's tools. Talk to a fee-only planner (not someone paid to sell you products). The $200K difference isn't random—it's the sum of a thousand small decisions starting today.

10/ Your claiming age isn't about maximizing a spreadsheet. It's about managing longevity risk, protecting your spouse, and aligning a government benefit with YOUR actual life. Get it right, and retirement breathes easy. Get it wrong, and you'll feel it for 30 years.
