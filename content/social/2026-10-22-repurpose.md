## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ You buy a duplex for $380K. Live in one unit. Tenant pays $1,700/mo. Your mortgage is $2,400. Suddenly your housing cost is $700 instead of $2,400—and you're building equity in a $380K asset.

That's house hacking. Not illegal. Not exotic. Just a strategy most first-time buyers have never heard of.

2/ House hacking = you live in part of a property and rent out the rest to offset your housing expense.

Classic models:
- Duplex/triplex (live in 1, rent others)
- Rent bedrooms in your home
- Convert basement/ADU to rentable space
- Airbnb spare rooms

3/ The FHA math is wild:
- 3.5% down on a 4-unit building
- Live in one unit, rent the others
- Lender counts 75% of projected rental income toward qualification
- Your housing cost plummets while you build equity

This is why FHA was created.

4/ Real example: Teacher, $54K salary, saved $20K over 3 years.

Buys $365K duplex with 3.5% down. Unit B rents for $1,600. Her housing cost drops from $1,650 → $1,716... except now she owns a $365K asset and builds $580/mo equity.

5/ The catch: FHA mortgage insurance ($240-300/mo) + you must live there 12 months minimum to avoid occupancy fraud + local zoning can ban rentals + landlord duties are real (leaky pipes at 11pm become your problem).

6/ Tax wins: Depreciation deductions, repairs, utilities, insurance, and mortgage interest (proportional) are all write-offs. Keep meticulous records.

7/ House hacking works best if you:
- Prioritize wealth-building over perfect privacy
- Can handle landlord duties (or afford a property manager)
- Plan to stay 12+ months
- Live somewhere with rental-friendly zoning

8/ After year one, move out and rent your unit. Potentially cover the entire mortgage with tenant income—or stay put and keep housing costs rock-bottom while you save for property #2.

9/ It's not flipping. It's not wholesaling. It's occupancy + leverage + time. The IRS and your lender both care deeply that you actually live there.

10/ First-time buyers now put down 9% average ($36K) and increasingly use house hacking to offset costs. Stop burning rent. Start building equity.
