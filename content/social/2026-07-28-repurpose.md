## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets Max)

```
1/ Most freelancers are leaving $30k/year on the table.

Why? They charge hourly, which caps income at time × rate, and doesn't account for admin work, taxes, or benefits.

Here's the pricing framework that changes everything.

2/ The hourly trap:
- Quote $75/hr
- Work 40 billable hours
- Spend 15 hours on admin (unbilled)
- Real rate: $54/hr
- After taxes & expenses: $33/hr

You're subsidizing clients' businesses with your own financial security.

3/ Project-based pricing flips the equation.

Same 8 hours of work.
Hourly quote: $600
Project quote: $2,500

Why? You're not selling hours—you're selling outcomes. A revenue-generating asset worth far more than your time.

4/ The 3-step formula:

Step 1: Calculate minimum monthly need
($80k salary ÷ 12 = $6,667 + 30% = $8,667/month)

Step 2: Decide client capacity (4-6 clients/month)

Step 3: Divide need by capacity
($8,667 ÷ 5 = $1,733/client minimum)

That's your floor.

5/ Value-based pricing is the final level.

Marketing consultant charges $12k for an email strategy overhaul that unlocks $180k in new revenue.

That's 6.7% of value created—a screaming deal for them, fair pay for you.

Only works when outcomes are quantifiable.

6/ Psychology matters: Price points signal quality.

$100-500: Budget clients, high maintenance, little loyalty
$1k-2.5k: Professional clients, respect expertise, repeat business
$5k+: Strategic clients, solving expensive problems

Aim for the middle tier.

7/ The litmus test:
If nobody ever says "that's expensive," you're undercharging.

Annual rate hikes: 5-10% for existing clients, 15-20% for new ones.
Inflation exists. Your skills improve. Your price should reflect that.

8/ Fix scope creep now:
Include 2 revision rounds in base price.
After that = 25-50% of project fee per round (or 1.5× hourly rate).

"Unlimited revisions" sounds generous.
It's actually a license for clients to work you for free.

9/ When they ask "What do you charge?":

Say: "For a project like this, my fee is $3,500. That includes strategy, two revisions, delivery in 3 weeks. Does that work?"

Then STOP TALKING.

The next person who speaks loses. Don't apologize. Don't discount immediately.

10/ Stop guessing your rates.

Calculate your minimum need.
Choose your pricing model (project or value-based).
Raise annually.
Protect your scope.

The math is simple. The psychology is harder.

But once you master it, you'll never underprice yourself again.
```
