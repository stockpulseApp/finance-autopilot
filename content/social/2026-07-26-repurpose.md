# Debt Consolidation Repurposing

## 1) X THREAD (10 Tweets Max)

```
1/ Debt consolidation looks great on paper: one payment, lower rates, cleaner finances. But millions of people use it wrong and end up deeper in debt. Here's the exact math and the traps that derail most people. 🧵

2/ The math that matters: consolidation only works if it lowers your *total interest paid* or speeds up payoff. "Simplicity" and "peace of mind" don't count. You need 5+ percentage points lower APR to justify the move.

3/ Real example: $15,100 in credit card debt at 22-25% APR = 18 years and $18,400 in interest if you pay minimums. Consolidate at 11.5% for 4 years? You pay $3,860 in interest. That's $14,540 saved.

4/ TRAP #1: Balance transfer cards with 0% APR look perfect—until month 19 when your remaining balance gets hit with 24.9% APR. Only use if you can pay off the *entire* balance during the promo window.

5/ TRAP #2: Using a home equity loan to consolidate credit cards converts unsecured debt into secured debt. Default on a credit card = dinged credit. Default on a HELOC = you lose your house.

6/ TRAP #3: Stretching the loan term to lower payments. $20k at 12% over 3 years costs $4k interest. Stretch it to 6 years and you pay $7.7k—an extra $3.7k just for lower monthly payments.

7/ TRAP #4: Origination fees (1-8%), balance transfer fees (3-5%), and settlement company fees (15-25%) can erase your savings entirely. Calculate net benefit: interest saved minus all fees. Anything under $500? Probably not worth it.

8/ The 5 scenarios where consolidation actually wins:
→ Significantly lower rate (5+ points)
→ You've fixed your spending problem
→ Can commit to full repayment term
→ Keeping old credit card accounts open
→ Your credit can handle the inquiry

9/ Before consolidating, ask yourself: Did I create this debt through overspending? If yes—consolidation treats the symptom, not the disease. You'll run up new debt while paying off the old loan.

10/ Consolidation is a tool, not a solution. It works when the numbers work AND your behavior changes. Get both right and you save tens of thousands. Get either one wrong and you're just delaying the real problem.
```

## REPURPOSING NOTES

**Key Angles Used:**
- Loss aversion (people ending up "worse off")
- Specific numbers ($14,540 saved, real examples)
- Trap/warning framing (4-5 specific mistakes)
- Contrarian angle (what ads don't tell you)
- Personal narrative potential (case studies like Jordan and Priya)

**Tone Maintained:**
- Data-driven, practical
- Skeptical but not alarmist
- Action-oriented (specific conditions, calculations)
- Longer-form for newsletter, punchy for social

All formats preserve the core message: *consolidation works under specific conditions, but most people ignore those conditions and get hurt.*
