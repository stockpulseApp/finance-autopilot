## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

```
1/ Debt consolidation saved Sarah $335/month. It trapped Marcus in $33k total debt.

The difference? One number: whether you'll spend like the problem is solved.

Here's when consolidation actually works—and when it destroys you.

2/ The math is seductive:
• $24k across 5 accounts at 20.3% avg rate
• Consolidate to single 8.5% loan
• Save $356/month, cut interest by $23.5k

But the psychology is where it breaks.

3/ ⚠️ Consolidation doesn't work if:
- You haven't fixed WHY you overspent
- You're converting unsecured debt to secured (risking your house)
- The "lower" rate isn't actually lower
- You extend the timeline so total interest INCREASES

4/ The consolidation sweet spot:
✅ New rate is 5%+ lower than weighted average
✅ Credit score 680+
✅ Stable income covers the payment
✅ You did the math on total cost (not just monthly payment)

5/ Marcus's cautionary tale:
Consolidated $22k → felt relief → racked up $11.3k NEW debt in 18 months → now owes $30.5k instead of $22k.

67% of consolidators without a budget repeat this pattern within 24 months.

6/ The credit score trap:
• Hard inquiry drops score 5-10 points immediately
• New account = lower average age of credit
• BUT: Paying on-time for 6+ months rebuilds it faster than minimum payments ever could

Temporary pain if you stay disciplined.

7/ Three consolidation methods ranked:
🥇 Personal loan (fixed rate, clean math, unsecured)
🥈 Balance transfer card (0% promo, but a deadline—risky)
🥉 Home equity (lowest rate, but secured by your house—risky)

8/ The real consolidation test:
Before you consolidate, answer this:
"If my minimum payment dropped $200/month, where would that money go?"

If it's "credit cards," stop. Fix the spending first.
If it's "savings/extra payments," proceed.

9/ The numbers that matter:
• If consolidation rate is 5%+ lower = likely worth it
• If extending timeline increases total interest = probably not
• If you have zero written budget = almost certainly backfires

Run the actual math. Don't guess.

10/ Consolidation is a tool, not a magic wand.

It works brilliantly for Sarah (disciplined, lower rate, fixed finish line).
It destroys Marcus (unaddressed spending, freed-up credit, no plan).

Know which one you are before you sign.
```
