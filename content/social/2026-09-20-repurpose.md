## 1. X Thread (10 Tweets)

1/ Most freelancers have a pricing STRATEGY problem, not a pricing problem. You're charging "what feels right" and leaving thousands on the table every year.

2/ Here's the math: if you want $60k take-home, add taxes (35%), health insurance, business costs. Divide by realistic billable hours (1,500/year). Minimum rate? $60+/hour. But that's just the floor.

3/ Cost-based pricing = time × hourly rate. Problem: you can't scale time. You work 60-hour weeks, burn out, resent clients. Solution: stop selling hours.

4/ Value-based pricing: What's the outcome worth to them? Developer built a checkout flow in 12 hours. At $75/hr = $900. Instead, he charged $8,500 (5% of $180k in new revenue the client gained). $708/hour.

5/ Outcome-based pricing takes it further: Marketing strategist charged $3k retainer + 3% of generated revenue. First month drove $95k in leads. Total fee: $5,850. Client paid gladly because ROI was crystal clear.

6/ Test this: Calculate your actual minimum rate using this formula—(Desired income + taxes + benefits + business costs) ÷ billable hours. Most people discover they're charging half what they need.

7/ Once you know your minimum? Charge 3x. Sounds insane. It's not. At 3x, you attract clients who value results over speed, can pick strategic projects, and actually have budgets.

8/ Jessica charged $45/hr copywriting, made $40k after taxes. Raised to $135/hr, lost 60% of clients temporarily. Then landed 3 dream clients. Worked 25 hrs/week, grossed $168k. Added coaching for another $45k.

9/ Pricing isn't one-time. As you gain experience, your rates evolve. Test your pitch out loud first—if you wouldn't say it face-to-face, simplify it to two sentences.

10/ Stop trading time for money. Start selling outcomes. Your skills are worth more than you think—once you believe it, clients will too.
