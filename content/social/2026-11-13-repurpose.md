```
1/ Most freelancers leave $30-60K on the table every year. Not because they lack skills. Because they price like employees, not business owners.

2/ The gap is brutal: One SEO consultant charges $2,499/hour. Most writers earn $5-10/hour after revisions. The difference? Pricing strategy, not talent.

3/ Here's why you're underpricing:
• You think billable hours = working hours (spoiler: they don't. Split your income in half)
• You're paying 15.3% self-employment tax + health insurance + software
• You're pricing TIME, not VALUE

4/ The real question isn't "What are my costs?"
It's "What is my end product worth to my customers?"

An email sequence that takes 12 hours isn't worth $600. If it generates $47K in sales, it's worth $3,500.

5/ Three pricing models that work:
VALUE-BASED: Charge based on client ROI
PROJECT-BASED: Fixed fee per deliverable
RETAINER: Monthly recurring revenue (the cash flow holy grail)

6/ The formula for your baseline rate:
(Target income + Business expenses) ÷ Realistic billable hours = Minimum rate

Then add $5-15/hour per year of experience. If you're specialized, add more.

7/ Example: Jamie wants $80K, estimates $32K in expenses. She'll bill 1,200 hours/year.
($80K + $32K) ÷ 1,200 = $93/hour minimum
Add 7 years experience: $133/hour

That's not aggressive. That's sustainable.

8/ One more reality check:
You're not billing 40 hours/week for 52 weeks.
Realistic: 20-25 billable hours/week when starting, 25-30 when established.
Account for admin, marketing, late payments, taxes.

9/ Your pricing compounds forever.
Raise your rate 10% this year? That's $5-10K more income. Same effort.
Stay underpriced? You're funding your client's payroll while building zero wealth.

10/ Stop racing to the bottom. Raise your rate. Document your results. Find clients who pay for value, not hours.

Your future self will thank you. 🧵
```
