## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

**Tweet 1:**
You landed the client. They ask "What do you charge?" Your stomach drops. Quote too high and they walk. Quote too low and you resent every hour. Here's how to stop guessing and start calculating. 🧵

**Tweet 2:**
Most freelancers price backward: they guess what clients might pay, then pray. Or they divide last year's salary by 2,080 hours—ignoring taxes, health insurance, unpaid admin time, and the fact that you won't bill 40 hours every week.

**Tweet 3:**
Your actual minimum hourly rate = (Annual living expenses + taxes + insurance + business costs) ÷ realistic billable hours.

Example: $65k needed ÷ 960 billable hours/year = $67.71/hour minimum. That's your floor. Period.

**Tweet 4:**
The cruel irony of hourly billing: the better you get, the less you earn per project. That logo took 12 hours at $70/hr ($840). Two years later, same logo takes 5 hours. Now it's worth $350 to you—even though it's better work.

**Tweet 5:**
This is why value-based pricing beats hourly. A logo isn't just pretty. It's the face of a business expecting $500k revenue this year. Charging $2,500 for that brand identity isn't expensive—it's a rounding error on the value created.

**Tweet 6:**
Case study: Priya left a $72k job charging $60/hr. After taxes & health insurance, she took home $2,200/month—less than her old job. She ran the math, jumped to $120/hr project pricing, and now grosses $95k/year from 12-15 projects.

**Tweet 7:**
Research what the market pays. Find 10-15 freelancers at your level. Check LinkedIn, portfolios, Upwork. If mid-level designers in your city charge $75-125/hr and you charge $35, you're not accessible—you're signaling your work is worth less.

**Tweet 8:**
Reverse-engineer value: What does your work enable the client to do? A sales page redesign that converts 3% instead of 1% = $50k extra revenue. Charging $5k for that is a bargain. Charging $500 is insulting.

**Tweet 9:**
Stop saying yes to "just one more thing" without charging. Stop lowering your price to keep a client. Stop giving expertise away free. Every time you do, you teach clients that's what you're worth.

**Tweet 10:**
The hard truth: charge 10x more, lose half your clients, still earn 5x more. 10 clients at $10k > 100 clients at $1k. Plus you get your life back. And when you charge fair rates, you make it possible for every other freelancer to do the same.
