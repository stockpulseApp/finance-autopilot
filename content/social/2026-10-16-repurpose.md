```
🧵 Most people save wrong. They max the 401(k) and call it a day.

But the order you fill tax-advantaged accounts matters — A LOT. We ranked them by real tax advantage per dollar. Here's the ladder:

1/ THE RANKING (from nuclear to solid):

🥇 HSA — triple tax advantage (pre-tax contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free medical withdrawals)
🥈 Roth 401(k) with employer match — free money meets tax-free growth
🥉 Roth IRA — flexibility meets compounding
4️⃣ Traditional 401(k) — upfront deduction, deferred tax
5️⃣ Traditional IRA — fallback only if you qualify
6️⃣ Taxable brokerage — after you've maxed the above

2/ WHY HSA WINS:

Most accounts give you ONE tax break. The HSA gives you THREE:
✅ Pre-tax contributions (reduce taxable income TODAY)
✅ Tax-free growth (compound for decades untouched)
✅ Tax-free withdrawals (for qualified medical expenses)

No other account matches this.

3/ THE SLEEPER MOVE:

Don't spend your HSA. Pay medical bills from checking, keep receipts, let the HSA invest and compound.

One example: Lena contributes $4,300/year starting at age 35. By 65, she has ~$435K tax-free. Same money in taxable? ~$310K.

The HSA wins by $125K.

4/ ROTH 401(k) BEATS ROTH IRA (for most):

Why? Contribution limits.
• Roth 401(k): $23,500/year
• Roth IRA: $7,000/year

Plus employer match is free money. Always capture the full match first — it's an instant 50-100% return before markets move.

5/ THE SEQUENCING MATTERS:

High earners who follow this ladder can accumulate $1M+ by retirement:
→ Max HSA first
→ Capture employer 401(k) match
→ Max Roth IRA (backdoor if you're over income limits)
→ Max Roth 401(k) deferral
→ Solo 401(k) if self-employed (mega backdoor Roth available)
→ Taxable brokerage

6/ SOLO 401(k) HACK:

If you're self-employed, a solo 401(k) lets you contribute $23,500 as employee + up to 25% of net self-employment income as employer (combined ~$69K in 2025).

And if your plan allows: mega backdoor Roth = an extra ~$45.5K to Roth per year.

7/ TAX DIVERSIFICATION:

Don't put everything in pre-tax accounts. You'll owe ordinary income tax on everything in retirement.

Mix:
• Pre-tax (traditional 401k/IRA)
• Post-tax (Roth 401k/IRA/HSA)
• Taxable (brokerage for flexibility)

This gives you control over retirement tax brackets.

8/ THE REAL PAYOFF:

Over 30 years, smart sequencing can mean $100K-$500K+ in extra wealth — just from *where* you saved, not *how much* you earned.

The ultra-wealthy know this. Now you do too.

Start with your HSA. Don't wait.

9/ NEXT STEPS:

1. Do you have a high-deductible health plan? If yes → open an HSA TODAY
2. Capture your full employer 401(k) match
3. Max Roth IRA (backdoor if needed)
4. Fill remaining 401(k) room
5. Open a taxable brokerage for anything beyond the limits

That's the ladder.
```
