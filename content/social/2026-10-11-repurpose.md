## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ A couple pulled $600K from stocks at the bottom. Watched the rebound happen without them. Their real mistake? No plan to get back in. Here's how dollar-cost averaging removes emotion from investing when markets feel terrifying. 🧵

2/ The hardest part of investing isn't picking funds. It's not panicking when your account turns red. 3 in 4 Americans expect a correction—and most will freeze when it happens.

3/ Enter dollar-cost averaging: invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of market price. $500/month every month. Up market, down market, sideways market. You invest.

4/ The math: When you buy at different prices, you naturally buy MORE shares when prices are LOW and FEWER when prices are HIGH. No prediction required.

5/ Example: $600/month over 3 months
- Month 1: $100/share → 6 shares
- Month 2: $75/share → 8 shares  
- Month 3: $90/share → 6.67 shares
Average cost: $87/share. Better than guessing.

6/ Yes, lump sum investments outperform DCA ~66% of the time. But that's not the real choice. The choice is: DCA or doing nothing. And most people choose nothing when scared.

7/ Priya kept $35K in cash "waiting for the right time." COVID crashed markets 34%. Instead of freezing, she split it into 12 monthly chunks. No heroics. Just systematic.

8/ Result: $35K → $68K by 2025. That's 94% growth. Not because she timed it perfectly. Because she actually invested instead of waiting.

9/ How to start: 
- Set a time frame (6-12 months)
- Divide your amount into equal chunks
- Automate monthly transfers + purchases
- Let psychology work FOR you, not against you

10/ The couple with $600K in cash? Keep 6-9 months expenses liquid. Invest the rest over 12-18 months. Automate it. Write down your plan. Then stop checking your account. Your future self will thank you.
