# Social Security Timing Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ The conventional wisdom: delay Social Security to 70 for max benefits. The reality: only 10% actually do it. Why? Because the "optimal" answer on a spreadsheet loses to real life—health, savings, job loss, and immediate needs. Let's talk actual trade-offs.

2/ The math is clean: 8% annual guaranteed return for waiting. File at 62 vs. 67? That's a permanent 30% monthly cut. File at 70? You get $2,480 instead of $1,400. But here's what calculators miss: can you actually afford to wait?

3/ Enter "breakeven age"—when delayed benefits finally catch up. Sounds smart. But it ignores: time value of money, portfolio opportunity costs, health uncertainty, and the simple fact that a retiree needing money NOW isn't making a math error—they're making a liquidity decision.

4/ Real case: Maya, 63, got laid off. Her portfolio took an 18% hit. Claiming at 63 ($1,610/mo) instead of 70 ($2,300/mo) preserved her savings and let her downshift to part-time work. Suboptimal by spreadsheet. Perfect for her actual life.

5/ Filing early makes financial sense when: shorter life expectancy, you're unemployed now, you can invest the difference, or you're married with different earnings (lower earner files early, higher earner delays—maximizes household cash flow + survivor benefits).

6/ Delaying to 70 wins when: still working (earnings test anyway), excellent health/longevity, can afford to wait without portfolio drain, or you want to maximize survivor/spousal benefits. The higher benefit also receives larger COLA increases—that compounds.

7/ Hidden gotcha: claim before 65 = auto-enrolled in Medicare at 65. Delay Social Security past 65? You must enroll separately or face permanent premium penalties. Coordinate this timing or you'll pay for it forever.

8/ The 90% claiming before 70 aren't statistical outliers—they're answering a different question. Not "how do I maximize lifetime benefits?" but "how do I keep my life stable *right now* while I still can?" Both questions are valid.

9/ The real answer: run *your* numbers with *your* health, *your* savings, and *your* timeline. Spreadsheet says 70? Ask yourself: can I really wait? Will I need it sooner? Do I have other income? The "right" age isn't universal—it's personal.

10/ Stop chasing the 8% return if it means financial stress today. Social Security is insurance + income. Use it when it actually insures your life. That might be 62. That might be 70. Both can be the right call.
