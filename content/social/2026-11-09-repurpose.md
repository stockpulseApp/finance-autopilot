# House Hacking Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

**Tweet 1:**
The median first-time homebuyer is now 40 years old. Housing costs have priced out an entire generation. But some buyers found a back door: house hacking. Buy a property, live in one unit, rent out the others. Let tenants pay your mortgage. Here's how it works. 🧵

**Tweet 2:**
House hacking = own a multi-unit property (duplex, triplex, fourplex) OR buy a house and rent out bedrooms OR convert a garage into an ADU. Live in one space, collect rent from the rest. Your net housing cost drops 50–70%.

**Tweet 3:**
Real example: $340K duplex in Chicago. Jordan puts down 5% ($17K), mortgage is $2,562/month. Rents the other unit for $1,400. Jordan's net housing cost? $1,162/month. That's $488 cheaper than renting a one-bedroom in the same area.

**Tweet 4:**
The financing advantage: FHA lets you put down 3.5% on up to a fourplex (if you occupy one unit). VA loans? Zero down for veterans. Conventional? 5–10% down. Compare that to investment property loans requiring 25% down. House hacking unlocks owner-occupied rates.

**Tweet 5:**
The catch: you must live there as your primary residence for at least 12 months. After that, you can move out and keep it as a rental. But you can't flip it immediately or you risk loan fraud.

**Tweet 6:**
Hidden costs everyone forgets: vacancy (budget 8.3%), maintenance (1–2% of property value annually), property management (8–10% of rent), capital expenditures (future roof/HVAC/water heater replacements), and potential eviction costs ($1,500–3,000).

**Tweet 7:**
Not everyone should house hack. You need: solid credit, emergency cash reserves, patience for tenant drama, ability to screen renters, and willingness to sacrifice privacy. If you just want a quiet home, this isn't for you.

**Tweet 8:**
Who it works best for: First-time buyers prioritizing wealth-building over lifestyle, people with VA loans, investors willing to self-manage, and buyers in Midwest markets where rent-to-price ratios favor landlords. Market matters.

**Tweet 9:**
The real payoff isn't the $488/month savings. It's that you're building equity on a $340K asset while renting does nothing. After 12 months, you move out, rent both units, and watch cash flow improve. That's how house hackers get ahead.

**Tweet 10:**
House hacking isn't a get-rich scheme. It's a deliberate entry strategy for buyers who refuse to let high rates and climbing prices lock them out of ownership. Trade privacy for wealth-building. It works—if you know what you're signing up for.
