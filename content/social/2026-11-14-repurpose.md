## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

```
🧵 Social Security timing: why the "break-even age" everyone quotes is a trap.

1/ You're 62. Ads say "wait until 70 for max benefits." Friends are cashing checks now. Who's right? The answer: both are, depending on YOUR situation—not generic rules.

2/ The math looks simple: claim at 62 = $1,505/mo. Wait until 70 = $1,968/mo. By 81 you break even. By 85 you're ahead. So delay, right?

Wrong. This ignores: cash flow needs, health reality, taxes, and portfolio returns.

3/ Meet Carla, 63: laid off, $180k in savings, $3,100/mo expenses. Waiting until 67 requires burning $148k from savings BEFORE first check arrives. She has no choice but to claim at 62—math says so.

4/ The "wait until 70" crowd assumes you have: ✓ Stable income ✓ Large savings ✓ Good health ✓ Life expectancy to 85+ This describes maybe 30% of retirees.

5/ Early claiming wins when: • Job loss + weak savings • Serious health issues • You want liquidity now (and can afford it) • You want to spend in your healthier 60s, not your 80s

6/ Delaying wins when: • Still earning good income • Longevity in family history • Large portfolio (can afford to wait) • Want to maximize survivor benefits (married couples) • Want inflation-proof income for life

7/ Hidden factor: taxes. Claiming early while drawing down IRAs can trigger higher taxes on benefits. Strategic Roth conversions in your 60s + delayed SS at 70 = sometimes better than conventional wisdom.

8/ Married couples especially: higher earner should usually delay to 70 (survivor gets that benefit). Lower earner can claim at 62-67. This isn't one decision—it's optimized math for two people.

9/ The real trap: letting break-even age be your only metric. You don't live in a spreadsheet. You live in a body that ages, jobs that end, and a future that's uncertain.

10/ Social Security timing deserves 5-10 hours of analysis with a CFP—not a Reddit thread. Your answer depends on YOUR numbers, YOUR health, and YOUR goals. Not Dave Ramsey's.
```
