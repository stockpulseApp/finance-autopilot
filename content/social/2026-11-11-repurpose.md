## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

1/ Debt consolidation sounds like a financial miracle: one payment, lower rate, debt gone. But 88% of people who consolidate end up back in debt. Here's why the math saves you money while behavior loses it. 🧵

2/ The numbers look great: $16,500 in credit card debt at 24% APR costs $33,900 total (14 years). Consolidate at 12% APR over 5 years? You pay $22,000. That's $11,900 saved.

3/ But here's the trap: After paying off those cards, they still work. Your credit limits are still there. Without fixing *why* you overspent, you'll rack up new balances while paying the consolidation loan.

4/ Result: $367/month consolidation payment + $7,000 back on credit cards = drowning twice as fast. Your old problem didn't disappear—you just got a new one.

5/ Consolidation only works if: (1) You have steady income, (2) You get a 5%+ rate drop, (3) You've built a spending plan, (4) You understand the total cost (fees included), (5) You can handle the new payment.

6/ Warning signs consolidation will backfire: Root problem is income vs. expenses (not just high rates) → Settlement/bankruptcy might be better → Using a home equity loan without 6 months saved → Poor credit means rates won't improve much.

7/ The hidden costs: Origination fees (1-6%), prepayment penalties, extended timelines = more total interest paid. Run the full math before signing.

8/ Credit score impact: Short-term pain (hard inquiry dip, utilization drop) for long-term gain. Keep cards open but don't use them. Make every payment on time. Over time, your score climbs.

9/ Real talk: If you can't afford your current expenses on your current income, consolidation is a band-aid. You need higher income or lower expenses. Preferably both.

10/ Bottom line: Consolidation is a *tool*, not a cure. It works when you've fixed the behavior. It fails when you haven't. Know which one you are before signing up.
