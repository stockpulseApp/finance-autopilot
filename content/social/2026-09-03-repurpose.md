# Social Security Timing Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ The couple had a $340k nest egg and a lake house they'd counted on as their safety net. 
They planned to delay Social Security to 70, maximizing their benefit to ~$5k/month.

Then the drought came. The cabin lost 60% of its value overnight. 🧵

2/ Forced to claim at 64 instead of 70, their monthly benefit dropped from $2,850 to $2,280—a permanent 20% cut they'll live with for potentially 30 years.

This isn't about bad planning. It's about real life throwing curveballs.

3/ Here's the math most people miss:
• Claim at 62: 30% permanent reduction
• Claim at 67 (FRA): Full benefit  
• Delay to 70: 24% INCREASE (8% annually)

That 8% guaranteed return beats any bond. But it only matters if you can afford to wait.

4/ The break-even trap: Financial planners obsess over what age you'd need to reach for delayed claiming to "win" in cumulative dollars. Usually around 80-81.

But that misses 3 critical things... (thread continues)

5/ LONGEVITY RISK: A woman claiming at 62 today has ~33% chance of reaching 90. Social Security is your only inflation-protected income. Waiting = stronger safety net against running out of money at 85+.

6/ SURVIVOR BENEFITS: For couples, the surviving spouse gets the *higher* benefit. Delay = widow/widower gets $2,900/month instead of $2,000. Over 20 years? That's $216k more.

7/ TAX EFFICIENCY: Claim early, spend down retirement accounts in low-tax years, let Social Security grow. Can actually increase your safe withdrawal rate and smooth lifetime taxes.

8/ When claiming early makes sense:
✓ Serious health condition (realistic <75)
✓ Unemployed, zero other income
✓ Lower earner in marriage (spouse delays)
✓ No assets, need immediate money

Get real about your situation.

9/ When delaying to 70 is worth it:
✓ Still earning good income
✓ Healthy family history of longevity  
✓ Spouse is higher earner (survivor benefit matters)
✓ Have enough assets to bridge 8 years

10/ Social Security timing isn't a math problem—it's a risk management problem.

Claiming early protects you if you die sooner than expected.
Delaying protects you if you live longer than your money.

Which risk matters more to YOU?
```
