## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets)

```
1/ Balance transfer cards can save you thousands in interest—or trap you in a deeper debt spiral. Here are the 5 critical traps smart people fall into (and how to avoid them). 🧵

2/ THE MATH THAT WORKS: $6,000 at 22% APR = $2,640 in interest over 48 months. Same debt on a 0% balance transfer card with a 3% fee = $180 total cost. That's $1,738 in savings—if you do it right.

3/ TRAP #1: THE TRANSFER FEE BLINDSPOT
Most people forget the 3-5% fee gets added to your balance day one. $10,000 transfer at 3% = $10,300 you're actually repaying. Your calculation needs to account for this or you'll miss the deadline.

4/ TRAP #2: THE NEW-SPENDING DISASTER
You open a new card, suddenly your old cards have available credit again. Then you use both. New purchases on balance transfer cards hit you with 18-27% APR immediately—while your payment goes toward the 0% balance. Don't do this.

5/ FIX: Lock the balance transfer card away. Physical drawer. Autopay only. If you need to spend, use a completely different card. Treat the transfer card like a fixed-term loan with one job: pay down transferred debt.

6/ TRAP #3: THE PROMOTIONAL WINDOW MIRAGE
18 months at 0% feels like forever. So you start at $400/month, drop to $300, then $250. Month 18 arrives—you still owe $3,285. APR hits at 24.99%. Plan to pay off in 19 months, not 18. Build in a 2-month buffer.

7/ TRAP #4: THE APPROVAL LIMIT SURPRISE
Card issuers won't approve unlimited transfers. Good credit gets you $10-15K. Fair credit gets you $2-5K. If you need to transfer $8,500 but only get approved for $7,000, the remaining $1,704 stays on your high-interest card accruing charges.

8/ FIX: Check your credit score before applying. Know your likely approval range. If you don't get enough credit, transfer the highest-APR balances first. A $6K transfer from a 26.99% card saves way more than the same amount from 18.99%.

9/ TRAP #5: THE SERIAL-TRANSFER TREADMILL
After you pay off one balance transfer, opening another card *looks* smart—but it dings your credit score, resets your debt payoff timeline, and keeps you on the hamster wheel. Break the cycle. Focus on building savings, not rotating debt.

10/ The bottom line: Balance transfers work—but only if you calculate transfer fees upfront, lock away the new card, plan for a 2-month early payoff buffer, understand your credit limit, and resist opening another card once you're debt-free. Do those 5 things. Win.
```
