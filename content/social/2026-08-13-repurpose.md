# Repurposed Content

## X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ Debt consolidation sounds like a magic wand—roll multiple debts into one lower-interest loan and boom, you're free. But it's not magic. Done right: saves you $18k+. Done wrong: doubles your debt. Here's when it works. 🧵

2/ The math is seductive. Rachel carries $22,400 across 4 credit cards at 24-28% APR. A consolidation loan at 11% cuts her payment from $673 to $488 and saves $18,600 in interest. Problem? She could re-rack those credit cards in 6 months.

3/ Consolidation works IF:
• You get a rate at least 5 points lower than your current average
• You have stable income to cover the new payment
• You've committed to NOT accumulating fresh debt
• You're tracking total cost, not just monthly payment

4/ The hard truth: consolidation doesn't erase debt. It just repackages it. If you're still overspending on discretionary items, you're not fixing behavior—you're just shuffling around a bigger financial problem.

5/ Credit score impact? Hard inquiry dips it 5–10 points temporarily. But lower utilization (paying off cards) can boost it 20+ points within 30 days. The net result is usually positive—IF you don't close old cards and crater your available credit.

6/ 🚩 Red flags: 
• Extending your repayment period (lower monthly payment ≠ better deal if you pay more total interest)
• Consolidating federal student loans (you lose income-driven repayment & forgiveness programs)
• Refinancing when your credit score is below 620

7/ The scenario that breaks people: consolidate, feel relief, slowly refill those paid-off credit cards. 18 months later? You have the consolidation loan AND new card debt. You've doubled your problem.

8/ Real talk: if you haven't fixed the spending behavior that created the debt, consolidation is expensive procrastination. The tool only works if you're committed to payoff—not just lower payments.

9/ The consolidation win requires three things: (1) lower interest rate, (2) stable income to sustain payments, (3) behavioral commitment. Get 2 out of 3 and you're gambling.

10/ Bottom line: Model the math. Compare total cost over life of loan. Make sure you can sustain the payment without refilling those cards. If all three check out, consolidation is a precision financial tool. If not, it's quicksand.
