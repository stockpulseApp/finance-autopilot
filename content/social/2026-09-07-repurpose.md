## 1) X THREAD (10 TWEETS MAX)

```
1/ You're paying $1,450/month rent. Your friend just bought a duplex with 3.5% down, lives in one unit, and tenants cover 70% of her mortgage. Here's the house hacking math nobody on TikTok explains.

2/ House hacking = Buy a 2–4 unit property with owner-occupant financing, occupy one unit, rent the others. Rental income offsets your mortgage by 40–70%. It's not a cheat code—it's forced savings wrapped in landlord responsibilities.

3/ The financing unlock: FHA loans let you buy with 3.5% down (vs. 15–25% for investment properties). 75% of projected rental income counts toward your debt-to-income ratio, even with zero landlord experience. That phantom income can swing qualification.

4/ The math: $285k duplex, $14.25k down, $2,150 mortgage. Tenant pays $1,800/month rent. Your net housing cost? $350/month. Compare that to renting at $1,450/month. That's $13,200/year in forced equity while you sleep.

5/ Five models exist: separate units (privacy + best math), ADUs (moderate rent, zone limits), rent-by-room (max income, zero privacy), short-term rentals (variable, insurance headaches), live-in flips (tax-free gains, construction chaos).

6/ The gotchas nobody mentions: vacancy reserves, maintenance at midnight, problem tenants, property tax increases, insurance premiums, and the legal obligation to screen applicants properly. You're not passive—you're now a small-business owner.

7/ FHA quirk: You must occupy the property as primary residence for 12 months minimum. Live elsewhere and you owe back the 3.5% down-payment advantage. Some lenders flag Airbnb as "business operation"—gray zone.

8/ Case study: Elena, 28, $82k salary, $22k saved. Duplex costs $20.75k to close. Mortgage $2,150. Tenant rent $1,800. Her net housing cost: $350/month vs. $1,450 renting. She gains $13.2k/year in equity while maintaining solvency.

9/ The real constraint isn't math—it's inventory. Duplexes are rare in hot markets. You'll compete with investors, lose to cash offers, or accept 45-minute commutes. Pittsburgh and Midwest metros have better duplex availability than coastal cities.

10/ House hacking works for buyers who trade privacy for equity and accept day-one landlord duties. Not a get-rich scheme. A wealth-building machine for people willing to delay gratification and answer tenant texts at 11 p.m.
```
