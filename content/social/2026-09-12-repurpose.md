Path A: Early & Active
✓ You have health concerns or shorter life expectancy
✓ You want to travel and enjoy retirement *now*
✓ You're still working (warning: earnings limits apply)
⚠️ You lock in a permanently reduced benefit

**Tweet 5**
Path B: Delay & Fortify
✓ You're in good health
✓ You have other income sources
✓ You're married and want to protect your spouse's survivor benefit
✓ You preserve portfolio value during critical early years

**Tweet 6**
Still working at 62? Earnings limit alert. Claim early and earn $36k while entitled to $2k/month? Social Security withholds $6,760 annually. The money isn't lost—your benefit recalculates up at full retirement age—but the cash-flow surprise is real.

**Tweet 7**
Married couples often leave six figures on the table by not coordinating claims. One spouse delays while the other claims at FRA. Result: larger survivor benefit + larger joint income in later years. Langan Financial Group warns: breakeven is typically age 80–82.

**Tweet 8**
The tax trap nobody talks about: Claim Social Security + take RMDs from your 401(k) in the same year = Medicare premiums spike. Combined income exceeds $32k (married)? Up to 85% of your benefits become taxable. Timing is everything.

**Tweet 9**
Real example: Priya (engineer) delays to 70 while Anand (teacher) claims at 67. Portfolio costs extra $43k ages 67–70. But when Anand dies at 82, Priya inherits *his higher benefit for life*. Over 15 years of widowhood: +$138k. Coordination wins.

**Tweet 10**
No optimal age exists. But a framework does: Model your health, lifespan, spouse, taxes, and portfolio risk. Then decide. Your Social Security decision is the most expensive blind spot in retirement planning. Don't leave $300k on the table.
