## 1. X THREAD (10 tweets max)

1/ Debt consolidation saved Sarah $16,000 in interest. But it also trapped her neighbor deeper in debt. Same tool, opposite outcomes. Here's how to know which one you'll get. 🧵

2/ The truth: consolidation isn't a magic fix. It's a tool that works brilliantly in some situations and catastrophically in others. The difference? Math + behavior.

3/ WHEN IT HELPS: You're paying 20%+ APR on scattered credit cards and can qualify for a loan at 10% or lower. That's real savings—but only if the new rate beats your current one by at least 3 percentage points.

4/ WHEN IT HELPS: You keep missing payments because you can't track 5 different due dates. One payment is harder to forget than five. Payment history = 35% of your credit score.

5/ WHEN IT HELPS: You have a plan. You've cut spending, built an emergency fund, and see consolidation as a bridge—not permission to keep spending. Without behavior change, you'll end up with the new loan + new credit card balances.

6/ WHEN IT HURTS: You haven't fixed the spending problem. Consolidate $15k and spend your way back to $15k in debt within 18 months? Now you're worse off. You've exhausted consolidation options.

7/ WHEN IT HURTS: A longer term kills you. That $12k at 22% APR costs $5,280 in interest over 4 years. Consolidate at 15% over 7 years and you pay $8,832—$3,600 MORE. Always calculate total interest paid.

8/ WHEN IT HURTS: Trading unsecured debt for a home equity loan means putting your house at risk. Credit card debt damages credit. Defaulting on a HELOC can cost you your home.

9/ THE CREDIT SCORE: Yes, there's a small temporary dip (hard inquiry + new account = 5-10 points). But if you pay off credit cards, your utilization ratio crashes and your score bounces back 40-80 points in 60 days.

10/ Bottom line: Consolidation works if (1) the math saves money, (2) you've fixed why you went into debt, and (3) you treat it as a bridge, not a restart button. Otherwise, it's just moving chairs on the Titanic.
