## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets)

```
1/ Market just dropped 8%. Your coworker sold everything. Your uncle stopped his 401(k) contributions.

You're opening your brokerage app, seeing red, and wondering: should I even keep investing right now?

The answer might surprise you. 🧵

2/ That nauseous feeling? Normal. That's your brain's loss-aversion kicking in—losses hurt ~2x more than gains feel good. CNBC is screaming. Everyone's panicking.

This is precisely when dollar-cost averaging becomes your superpower.

3/ Dollar-cost averaging = investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of market conditions. Monthly. Biweekly. Whatever.

It's boring. And when volatility spikes, boring = competitive advantage.

4/ Real example: Priya invests $500/month into an S&P 500 fund over 6 months of volatility.

When prices drop to $160, she buys MORE shares.
When prices rise to $210, she buys FEWER shares.

Same $3,000 invested. More shares acquired. Lower average cost.

5/ The research paradox: Lump-sum investing outperforms DCA ~67% of the time over 10-year periods.

BUT if you panic-sell after a 30% crash, that advantage disappears. DCA's slightly lower expected return is insurance against your own behavior.

6/ Here's what research actually shows:
- If you have strong emotional discipline → lump sum wins
- If you're nervous during downturns → DCA keeps you invested
- Most people overestimate their discipline

Choose wisely.

7/ When DCA works best:
✓ You're already doing it (401k contributions)
✓ Valuations feel stretched + your conviction is shaky
✓ You're in the middle of a correction
✓ You're a beginner building confidence

8/ Implementation is simple:
→ Set it up once
→ Automate everything (most brokerages offer this)
→ Increase contributions with raises
→ DO NOT check prices before buying

The entire point is removing discretion.

9/ Market corrections happen ~1x/year. Bear markets ~1x/3.5 years.

An investor who keeps buying during corrections locks in better long-term expected returns than someone who sold during panic.

History is clear: selling at the bottom is costliest mistake.

10/ Bottom line: Your brain wants you to stop investing during downturns. Don't listen.

Set up DCA. Automate it. Keep buying when headlines terrify you.

That's how wealth builds. One boring, consistent contribution at a time.
```
