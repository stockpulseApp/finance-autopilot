# Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets Max)

1/ Most freelancers don't have a skills problem. They have a pricing strategy problem. Charging "what feels right" costs you $20k-$50k annually in lost income. Here's how to fix it. 🧵

2/ The base formula: (Monthly expenses + Income goal) ÷ Billable hours = Your floor rate. Example: $6,000 needed ÷ 80 billable hours = $75/hr. But that's just the start—multiply by 1.5x for taxes and overhead. Real minimum: $112.50/hr.

3/ Six pricing models beat hourly rates every time:
• Project-based (flat fee)
• Value-based (client ROI)
• Tiered packages (choice without confusion)
• Retainers (predictable income)
• Revenue-share (upside potential)
• Outcome-based (results matter)

4/ Value-based pricing example: Your content strategy increases client signups by 100/month. That's $47k+ in new annual revenue. Charge 10% of projected value ($4,752) not your hours. Price reflects impact, not effort.

5/ Tiered packages give clients choice while keeping you in control. Starter → Core (your sweet spot) → Premium. Psychological anchor: The middle option feels reasonable. The premium makes the core seem like a deal.

6/ The retainer advantage: $4k/month beats $50/hr freelancing. You get predictable income, reduced sales stress, deeper relationships. Define deliverables clearly though: "4 articles/month, 2 revisions max" beats "content stuff."

7/ Red flags your rates are too low:
• Clients accept instantly every time
• Fully booked but still struggling financially
• Haven't raised rates in 18+ months
• Peers charge 30-50% more for same work

Time to raise your rates.

8/ The negotiation script: "Based on the scope, business impact, and market rates for this level of expertise, my investment is [amount]. I'm confident this delivers strong ROI." Data beats apologies. Value beats humility.

9/ Most common mistake: Tying income to time. Hourly rates penalize you for getting better—fewer hours = less pay. Shift to project or value-based pricing. Reward efficiency. Reward expertise. Reward yourself.

10/ Stop leaving money on the table. Use a pricing model that fits your work, calculate your true floor, and charge accordingly. Your future self (and your retirement account) will thank you.
