# Social Security Timing Repurposed Content

## X Thread (10 Tweets)

1/ The $400K question nobody wants to answer: claim Social Security at 62 or wait until 70? The decision literally swings your lifetime benefits by $400,000+. Here's the math that matters. 🧵

2/ The central tension is brutal: retire early for reduced benefits NOW, or delay for a bigger check LATER? Early income feels good today. But delayed benefits often matter more in later years when other income dries up.

3/ Meet Sarah: earning $85K at 62, eligible for $1,260/month in Social Security. If she claims early while working, the earnings test withholds $1 for every $2 above $24,480. Her benefit? Nearly wiped out until she hits full retirement age (67).

4/ Little-known fact: full retirement age is now locked at 67 for anyone born in 1960+. That's a steeper early-claiming penalty than your parents faced. Claim at 62? You get just 70% of your benefit. Wait until 70? You get 124%. The gap: $1,080+/month for life.

5/ The break-even myth: most analyses say delaying pays off around age 78-82. But they ignore survivor benefits. If you're married, waiting locks in a higher benefit your spouse inherits for LIFE. That can add $150K+ to their lifetime income.

6/ Inflation is your friend in delayed claiming. A larger base benefit means bigger COLA adjustments every year. Over 20 years, those compound into a widening gap. Live to 85? Delaying until 70 becomes a home run.

7/ Have a pension? That changes everything. If pension income covers basics, you can delay Social Security until 67 or 70 and maximize your benefit. Pensions don't adjust for inflation—Social Security does. Delay locks in protection.

8/ Federal employees: the FERS Supplement bridges the gap until Social Security at 62. Retire early, collect supplement + pension, delay Social Security until 70. That's the optimal sequence for most scenarios.

9/ The real question isn't "when do I break even?" It's "how do I sequence my retirement income to avoid running out in my 80s and 90s?" Claiming early depletes portfolio faster when you're young. Delayed claiming protects you when market risk + health shocks hit hardest.

10/ Bottom line: there's no universal right answer. But if you have a pension, a spouse, or expect to live past 82, delaying Social Security is likely worth $100K-$400K+ over your lifetime. Run the numbers with your specific situation. Don't guess.
