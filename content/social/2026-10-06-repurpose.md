## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

```
1/ Debt consolidation gets sold as financial salvation. Lower rate, one payment, problem solved. 

But here's the hard truth: it works brilliantly for some people and destroys others. The difference? Math most people skip.

Let me break down when consolidation actually helps.

2/ What consolidation IS: Rolling multiple debts into one loan/card, ideally with a lower interest rate. You're reorganizing debt, not erasing it.

What it's NOT: Bankruptcy, settlement, or free money. You still owe 100% of what you borrowed.

3/ Meet Sarah (credit score 710): $18k in credit card debt at ~22% APR. Consolidated into one loan at 11.5% over 5 years.

Result: Cut monthly payment from $478 → $397. Saved ~$15k in interest. Clear finish line.

This worked because her rate was MEANINGFULLY lower.

4/ Meet James (credit score 640): $14k debt at 21% APR. Consolidated at 15.5% with a 5% origination fee ($700).

Marginal savings. Then he racked up $6k in NEW debt because he treated paid-off cards as "permission to spend."

Now $21k in debt. Score tanked.

5/ The critical rule: Consolidation only works if total cost is LOWER than your current trajectory AND you don't change spending behavior.

Most people fail on the second part.

6/ The math you need:
- Calculate what you'd pay if you kept current debts
- Calculate consolidation total (including ALL fees)
- Subtract
- If savings < $1k or break-even > 18 months, skip it

7/ Consolidation makes sense if:
✓ You qualify for 5+ percentage points lower
✓ Your credit score is 680+
✓ You've fixed whatever caused the debt (job is stable, emergency fund exists)
✓ You're making only minimums and getting nowhere

8/ Consolidation doesn't help if:
✗ Your rate drop is less than 5 points
✗ Origination fees kill early savings
✗ You have zero spending discipline
✗ Your income is unstable
✗ You're only moving the problem around

9/ Hard truth: Consolidation is a tool for people with stable lives who need organization, not a tool for people whose lives are unstable.

If you're considering it, ask yourself: "Have I actually fixed the problem that created this debt?"

If not, consolidation will fail.

10/ The real first step isn't consolidation—it's understanding WHY you're in debt. Emergency? Overspending? Both?

Fix the root cause first. Then consolidation becomes a legitimate leverage tool instead of a band-aid on a broken financial life.
```
