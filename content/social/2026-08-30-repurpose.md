## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets Max)

1/ Balance transfer cards saved people like Amara $1,868 in year one. But they also trap thousands deeper in debt. Here's the actual math on whether they're your financial lifeline or quicksand.

2/ The setup: You transfer high-interest debt (22% APR killing you) to a new card with 0% for 6-21 months. The fee? 3-5%. Sounds simple. The execution? That's where most people fail.

3/ Quick math: $12,400 balance at 22% costs $2,728 in interest year one. Transfer it for a 3% fee ($372)? You save ~$2,356 in 18 months even before the promo ends. Break-even takes weeks, not months.

4/ TRAP #1: Credit limit theater. Approved for $10K? You can't transfer $10K—the fee eats into your limit. You're looking at ~$9,700 max. And if your total debt is $30K? You're only moving a fraction.

5/ TRAP #2: The spending relapse. Old cards now show available credit. New card lets you spend. Both feel "free." Suddenly you're racking up new charges at 24% APR while your 0% balance sits there untouched. Debt increases, not decreases.

6/ TRAP #3: Minimum payment math. Pay the minimum on that $9.5K balance over 18 months? You'll owe $6,080 when 0% expires. That $6,080 now accrues 24% interest. You just rearranged the furniture, didn't leave the building.

7/ SMART MOVE: Calculate your target payment before applying. $6K balance ÷ 15 months = $400/month required. Set it as automatic payment. Non-negotiable, like rent. Your promotional window is a *structured opportunity*, not a grace period.

8/ TRAP #4: The reversion rate ambush. Your 0% ends. New APR jumps to 23.99%. If you still owe $6K and make minimum payments? Add another $3,800+ in interest. You're not debt-free; you're on a new payment plan.

9/ The fundamental test: Will this actually reduce your *total debt*, or just shuffle it? If you're transferring to stop the bleeding and commit to a payoff plan, it works. If you're buying breathing room to keep spending, you're worse off than before.

10/ Real talk: Balance transfers work when paired with a zero-based payoff calculation, frozen old cards, and psychological discipline. Without those three? They're debt acceleration tools disguised as relief.
