## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
🧵 Balance transfer cards can save you $5,000+...or trap you in worse debt. 
Here's what most people get wrong.

1/ The math looks good: $20K at 24% APR = $6,200 in interest over 18 months.
Move it to 0% APR? You save ~$5,000, pay only a 3-5% fee ($600-1K).
But the catch? You have to actually pay it off. Miss the deadline = 20%+ revert rate. 💀

2/ TRAP #1: The transfer fee isn't optional
$20K transfer @ 3% = $600 fee added to your balance.
Worth it? Only if the interest saved is bigger.
Transferring $1K? The fee eats most of your savings. Only do it if you need 6+ months to pay off.

3/ TRAP #2: New purchases aren't free
That new card feels like breathing room. You buy a couch. Book a trip. Suddenly you're adding $3K in debt while paying down old debt.
Pro tip: Lock the card away. Use cash or debit for everything else.

4/ TRAP #3: The revert rate is brutal
You transfer $10K, make "decent" payments for 18 months, still owe $4K when 0% expires.
Revert rate hits 22%+. You're back in the trap.
The transfer only wins if you clear the balance *before* the intro period ends.

5/ SMART MOVE: Do the math first
Divide your total balance by intro months. That's your required payment.
Can't afford it? The transfer won't save you—it'll just delay the pain.
Example: $15,080 ÷ 18 months = $838/month. Non-negotiable.

6/ Your credit score determines everything
Need excellent credit for the longest intro periods + lowest fees.
FICO 700+? Best offers available.
FICO <670? Shorter windows, higher fees. Work on your score first if you're not there yet.

7/ The real test: can you change your spending?
Balance transfers only work if you stop accumulating new debt.
It's not about moving money around—it's about behavioral change + realistic payoff plan + commitment.
Without all three, you're just buying time.

8/ Real example: Jessica transferred $14.5K, cut subscriptions, meal-prepped, took a side gig to hit $838/month.
18 months later: $0 debt, saved $12K+ in interest, avoided 10+ years of payments.
Alternate ending: kept the old spending, still trapped.

9/ The key question before applying:
Can I realistically afford the required monthly payment to clear this balance in the intro period?
If the answer is "maybe" or "probably not," skip the transfer. Attack the debt with your current card instead.

10/ Balance transfers work when you have:
✅ High-interest debt (19%+ APR)
✅ Large enough balance that fees < interest savings
✅ Stable income + realistic budget
✅ Commitment to stop spending during payoff window
✅ Clear plan to hit zero before revert rate kicks in

Hit all five? It's a game-changer.
```
