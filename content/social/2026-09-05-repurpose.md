## 1) X THREAD (10 Tweets Max)

1/ Markets dropping 8%? Your instinct screams "STOP INVESTING." That's exactly when dollar-cost averaging wins.

DCA = investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, no timing, no guessing. When prices fall, you buy more shares. When they rise, you buy fewer. Simple.

2/ The hard truth: professional investors fail at market timing. A 2023 DALBAR study found the average investor underperformed the S&P 500 by 3.5% annually over 30 years—mostly from poorly timed buys and sells.

If the pros can't do it, why bet your retirement on it?

3/ The math is elegant. Invest $6k all at once? You might buy 60 shares at $100 each. Spread it over 6 months? You could end up with 65+ shares at a lower average cost. Volatility becomes your advantage, not your enemy.

4/ Real example: Sarah invested $400/month starting in 2019. When COVID crashed markets in March 2020, she kept going. That $400 was buying shares 25% cheaper. By 2025, her account hit $38k—not from timing perfectly, but from never stopping.

5/ DCA isn't about maximizing returns (lump-sum investing actually wins ~2/3 of the time). It's about maximizing the odds you'll *stay invested*. Behavioral edge beats mathematical edge.

6/ Bear markets last ~9.5 months on average. By the time markets "feel safe" again, they've already recovered 10-15%. You've missed the snapback while waiting for the all-clear signal that never comes.

7/ Implement it in 2 steps:
1) Pick your amount ($100-$1k per period)
2) Automate it

Then delete the app. Check it once per year. That's it.

8/ Your 401(k)? Already dollar-cost averaging. Every paycheck, a fixed amount goes in. You're not checking if markets are scary before you contribute. You just... contribute.

Apply the same logic to your brokerage account.

9/ The real risk isn't volatility. It's stopping during volatility. DCA removes that decision. You're not trying to be clever. You're showing up, consistently, whether headlines scream "CRASH" or "RECOVERY."

10/ Stop timing. Start investing. Same amount. Same schedule. Every time.
