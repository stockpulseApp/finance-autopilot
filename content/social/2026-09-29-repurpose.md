## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets)

```
1/ You quit your $72k job to freelance and charged $50/hour. 

Six months later: 55-hour weeks, $58k/year, broke and burned out.

The problem? You did simple division instead of real math.

Here's how to actually price freelance work:

2/ Your salary was NOT $60k. Your employer paid ~$78k when you add benefits, taxes, PTO, and insurance.

As a freelancer, YOU cover all that.

That $29/hour "equivalent" actually needs to be $92/hour minimum to match your old life.

3/ Start here:
- Target annual income
- Add 25-30% for self-employment tax
- Add health insurance, retirement, expenses
- Divide by realistic billable hours (NOT 2,080)

Most freelancers bill 25-30 hours/week, not 40.

The math changes everything.

4/ Hourly billing = poison.

It rewards slowness, punishes efficiency, and traps you explaining why you got faster at your job.

Quit hourly rates immediately.

5/ Better model #1: PROJECT PRICING
Quote flat fees with a 20-30% buffer.
If you finish fast? Bonus profit.
If scope creeps? You're still protected.

Clients love knowing total cost upfront.

6/ Better model #2: VALUE PRICING
Your marketing consult generates $100k in new revenue for the client?

Charge $8-15k. Not based on hours—based on their outcome.

Charge based on the pain the client feels, not the hours you logged.

7/ Better model #3: RETAINERS
$3,500/month for ongoing content = recurring income + predictable cash flow.

Long-term relationships reward you both.

Retainers scale your income without scaling your hours.

8/ Pro move: SPECIALIZE
"Freelance writer" competes with millions at $15/hour.

"B2B email sequences for HR tech companies" charges 40-50% more.

Narrow your niche = raise your rates.

9/ The platform trap:
Upwork/Fiverr take 20% commission AND encourage a race to the bottom against $15/hour global competition.

Use platforms to build your first portfolio, then transition clients off-platform to your actual rates.

10/ Your rate isn't arbitrary.
It's the math of covering taxes, benefits, and actually getting paid for your expertise.

Calculate it. Own it. Charge it.

Stop leaving money on the table.
```
