## X Thread (10 Tweets)

1/ Most budgets fail by March. The problem isn't willpower—it's design. Traditional budgeting asks you to track what you spent last month and promise to do better. Zero-based budgeting flips it: assign a job to every dollar *before* the month starts. It's a game-changer.

2/ Zero-based budgeting means: Income - All Planned Expenses = $0. Exactly. Every dollar gets assigned: rent, groceries, retirement, emergency fund, whatever. You're not spending everything—you're "spending" on savings too. The psychological shift matters.

3/ The Four Walls framework tells you what to fund FIRST when money's tight:
1️⃣ Food (groceries)
2️⃣ Shelter (rent/mortgage)
3️⃣ Utilities (heat, water, power)
4️⃣ Transportation (car to work)

Only after these four, everything else.

4/ Start by calculating your actual take-home income. If it varies, use your lowest month from the past 6. This sounds pessimistic. It also means you won't plan to spend $4K in a month you only earn $3.2K.

5/ List every recurring bill in Four Walls order. Then honestly estimate variable expenses—groceries, gas, household items. Use your highest month from the past 3 and add 10%. Don't create an aspirational budget. Document reality.

6/ Assign savings and debt repayment to what's left. Emergency fund first (under $1K), then high-interest debt above minimums, then full emergency fund (3-6 months), then retirement, then other goals. If you hit zero before getting to all of them? You just found your real constraint.

7/ Whatever's left goes to discretionary: restaurants, entertainment, hobbies, clothes. If nothing's left, you can't afford these yet. This is the magic of zero-based budgeting—it surfaces hard truths instead of letting you wonder where money disappeared.

8/ Real example: Sarah earns $4,800/month. Fixed + variable expenses = $3,385. After assigning $600 to savings/debt, she has $815 for discretionary. That's her real number. She can't have $1,000 restaurants without cutting savings. The budget doesn't lie.

9/ Maintain it in under 10 minutes/week. Use an app that auto-syncs transactions (YNAB, EveryDollar) OR a simple spreadsheet. Don't log every $1.87 transaction. Just check: are you staying in category ranges? If yes, you're winning.

10/ Zero-based budgeting isn't about restriction—it's clarity. When you know your actual constraints, you can make real tradeoffs instead of wondering why you're broke. That clarity is what makes budgets actually stick.
