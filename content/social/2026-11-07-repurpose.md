# Repurposed Content: Dollar-Cost Averaging

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

1/ The market just dropped 8%. Your coworker says "pull everything out." Your stomach turns. This is when dollar-cost averaging earns its keep. It's not about beating the market—it's about staying in the game when fear would sideline you completely.

2/ Here's the thing: lump-sum investing usually wins mathematically. Fidelity's research shows it outperforms ~66% of the time in rising markets. But most people don't have a lump sum sitting around. They have a paycheck. Monthly. Regular. Predictable.

3/ Real talk from Reddit's investing community: "Dollar-cost averaging isn't a choice between strategies—it's the only option most people have." You invest $X from each paycheck. That's DCA. That's normal investing.

4/ Case study: Ethereum peaked at $4,946 in Aug 2025. Two investors, $5,000 each.
- Investor A: $5k lump sum at peak → now worth $2,450 (down 51%)
- Investor B: $100/week for 50 weeks → lower average cost, less damage

Systematic buying during declines = automatic "buy the dip"

5/ Meet Rachel, 34. She invested $500/month starting Jan 2022—right before one of the worst stock market years. By Oct 2022, she was down 24%. Her roommate panicked and pulled out. Rachel kept investing.

Result: $18k invested over 3 years = $23.4k account balance (+30%)

6/ The math vs. the mind: Research shows investors are loss-averse. A $50k lump sum dropping 15% = $7.5k loss = panic sell. That same $50k invested $2k/month? A 15% drop in month 3 = $900 loss on deployed capital = you stay the course.

7/ Morgan Stanley: "It's easy to become paralyzed by decisions over exactly how and when to invest." DCA removes that paralysis. Can't time the market? Buy consistently. Market drops? You're buying more shares cheaper. Market rises? You're still in it.

8/ When DCA makes sense:
✓ Investing from regular income (401k, monthly investments)
✓ You have $40k to invest but market timing scares you
✓ In a documented bear market (buying at lower prices)

When it doesn't:
✗ $100k lump sum, 35+ year horizon (deploy it all)
✗ Need the money in 2 years

9/ The real advantage: Not returns. Staying power. Peace of mind. A structured system that keeps emotion out of decisions. You remove the question "What if it crashes after I invest?" because the answer is "I'll buy more next month at the lower price."

10/ Bottom line: You probably don't need to *choose* DCA vs. lump-sum. If you're earning regular income and investing it, you're already doing DCA. If it feels scary, that's the point—this strategy works *because* it feels boring enough to stick with.
