1/ Most budgets fail because they're backward-looking guilt trips. 
You track what you *spent*, feel bad, repeat next month.

Zero-based budgeting flips the script: plan forward, get specific, and actually stick.

Here's how one person paid off $6.8K debt + saved $9K:

2/ The core principle: Income minus all assigned expenses = zero.

Not "spend everything" zero—"accounted for everything" zero.

If you bring home $4,200, you decide what all $4,200 does BEFORE you spend a dime.

3/ Step 1: Calculate true take-home (not gross salary)
Step 2: List every fixed + variable expense 
Step 3: Prioritize financial goals as actual budget line items
Step 4: Assign every dollar until it equals zero

4/ The magic happens in Step 4.

When you force yourself to account for every dollar, you either:
- Trim goals temporarily
- Increase income
- Cut expenses

There's no floating "oops, where'd that go" money.

5/ When you spend $43 at groceries out of your $450 budget, you see the remaining $407.

One is a guideline. The other is a plan.

You have a structural problem—rent/car too high.

You need to increase income or reduce major costs.

8/ Track spending 2x/week minimum.

9/ The payoff isn't just money saved.
