# Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ Most freelancers price wrong. They Google "average rates," shave off 20% because they're unsure, then wonder why they're broke after 5 years. Stop anchoring to other people's survival math. #freelance

2/ Here's the fix: Calculate backward from what you need to earn. Need $8K/month? Can serve 4 clients well? That's $2K minimum per client. If you can't hit that rate, it's not a pricing problem—it's positioning, skill, or targeting. Do the math first.

3/ Hourly billing kills your income growth. A logo that takes 8 hours in year 1 takes 2 in year 3—you're 4x better. But you just lost 75% of the revenue. Speed = penalty. That's backwards.

4/ Minimum hourly rate math: (Annual expenses + desired profit) ÷ 1,200 billable hours/year. Most assume 2,000 hours. You have 25 billable/week, not 40. Factor in no paid leave, no health insurance, taxes. You need 1.5x a salaried employee's hourly rate just to break even.

5/ Project-based pricing shifts the conversation from "how long?" to "what does this accomplish?" A website redesign that lifts conversions 50% isn't expensive at $5K—it's a steal at $15K. Always offer 3 tiers (lean/standard/premium). Clients anchor to the middle one.

6/ Value-based pricing sounds scary but makes the most money. A SaaS strategist I know was charging $3K for content plans. Then a client revealed they were losing $50K/month on poor conversion. She pitched $12K. She moved 8% → 13%. Client renewed at $15K. Same effort, different impact.

7/ Try base fee + performance bonus: charge lower base to reduce client risk, earn a % of measurable outcomes. Client wins $60K? You get $1,200 base + 3% bonus = $3K total. Aligns incentives. Both sides focused on results, not hours burned.

8/ Specialization beats discounting. A developer in poverty countries out-earned competitors by specializing in fintech—not competing on price. Healthcare, legal tech, cybersecurity: niches support premium rates because generalists can't do the work. Pick a lane.

9/ Competing on price is stupid. You lose. Instead: specialize → get results → raise rates → specialize deeper. Kyle Prinsloo went $0 → $200 → $500 → $1K → $5K+ websites. Each jump matched skill improvement + proven results.

10/ Stop undercharging. You don't have a confidence problem. You have a positioning problem. Know your niche, know your value, know your floor. Charge it.
