## 1) X Thread (10 tweets)

```
1/ Debt consolidation saved Elena $10,100. It nearly cost Jordan his house.

The difference? Understanding when this tool works—and when it's a trap.

Here's the real math lenders don't want you to see. 🧵

2/ The appeal is obvious: $22k across 4 credit cards at 24% APR = $440/month just in interest.

Consolidate into 1 loan at 13.5%? You're suddenly paying yourself instead of the bank.

But here's where people go wrong...

3/ ✅ CONSOLIDATION WORKS when:
• Your rate drops 3-5+ points
• You have steady income
• You stop using credit cards
• You're organized enough to avoid new debt

If any of these fail? You're rearranging deck chairs on a sinking ship.

4/ ❌ THE BIGGEST TRAP:

You consolidate $18k, then keep spending $1k/month MORE than you earn.

18 months later? You have the personal loan PLUS $12k in new credit card debt.

Consolidation doesn't fix behavioral leaks. It just hides them temporarily.

5/ THE HIDDEN FEES:
• Origination fees (1-8%)
• Balance transfer fees (3-5%)
• Annual fees

On an $8k consolidation at 6% origination fee ($480), it takes 15 months just to break even. Close that card too early and you lost money.

6/ CREDIT SCORE WARNING:

Hard inquiry = 5-15 point temporary drop.
Closing old cards = higher utilization ratio = more damage.
New account = lower average age = longer recovery.

But here's the good news: on-time payments rebuild it in 3-6 months.

7/ THE $40K+ THRESHOLD:

If you're drowning in $40k+ of high-interest debt, consolidation alone might not save you.

Alternatives:
• Debt settlement (negotiate balances down 40-60%)
• Debt management plans (nonprofit agency negotiates for you)
• In extreme cases: bankruptcy

8/ GOLDEN RULE:

Never consolidate into secured debt (home equity loans) unless your income is ROCK SOLID.

Jordan did this. Freelance work dried up. He missed payments. Now facing foreclosure on a house to protect credit card debt.

Unsecured > risking your home.

9/ THE DECISION CHECKLIST:

□ Interest rate drops 3-5+ points?
□ Credit score 660+?
□ Monthly payment affordable on worst-case income?
□ Will you actually stop using cards?
□ Fees justified by interest savings?

If you can't check all 5: consolidation isn't your answer.

10/ Bottom line: Consolidation is powerful IF you're disciplined.

It's deadly if you're just extending a spending problem.

Before you apply, ask yourself: "Will consolidating fix my debt, or just hide it?"

Your answer determines everything.
```
