## 1) X THREAD (10 tweets max)

1/ Debt consolidation sounds perfect: one payment, lower rate, financial peace. But here's the truth—it can save you $9,000+ OR trap you deeper in debt. The difference? Math + behavior. Here's what actually matters before you consolidate.

2/ The mechanics are simple: combine multiple debts into ONE loan at ONE rate. Usually 2 types:
• Personal loan (fixed 7-25% APR, 2-7 yr term)
• Balance transfer card (0% APR for 12-21 months + 3-5% fee)
The goal? Cut interest. Simplicity is secondary.

3/ WHEN IT WORKS: Meet Jordan, $18k credit card debt across 4 cards at ~22% APR. Minimum payments? $540/mo + $15,200 in interest over 18 years.

Consolidate at 11.5% APR? $399/mo, $5,940 total interest, paid off in 5 years. Save: $9,200.

4/ Consolidation wins when:
✓ You're paying 18%+ APR
✓ Minimums barely touch principal
✓ You have steady income
✓ You've FIXED the spending that created the debt (critical)

Without fixing behavior, you'll just run up new debt on cleared cards.

5/ THE TRAP: You consolidate $15k in credit card debt, feel relief seeing $0 balances, then gradually charge $8k more. Now you're paying BOTH the consolidation loan AND new credit card debt. You haven't reduced debt—you've increased it.

6/ Other hidden traps:
🚨 Trading credit card debt for home equity debt = risking your house
🚨 7-year terms = paying MORE interest despite lower rates
🚨 Fees (3-8%) eat your savings before you even start
🚨 One missed payment destroys your credit + triggers penalty rates

7/ The math can be beautiful or brutal. Before consolidating, ask:
1) Am I genuinely cutting my interest rate?
2) Have I fixed my spending habits?
3) Did I calculate total fees into my savings?
4) Can I handle the payment without new debt?

8/ Consolidation is a tool, not a cure. It works for people with steady income, fixed spending, and genuine math advantage. It backfires for people using it to avoid the real problem: lifestyle inflation.

Know which one you are.

9/ Scenario: You consolidate at 12% over 5 years vs. aggressively paying at 20% over 3 years. Lower rate feels better. But 5-year payment? You might pay MORE total interest. Run both numbers. Sometimes the uncomfortable payment is smarter.

10/ Bottom line: Consolidation helps when the numbers favor you AND you've addressed the behavior that created the debt. Miss either piece? You're not consolidating—you're delaying. Don't do that.
