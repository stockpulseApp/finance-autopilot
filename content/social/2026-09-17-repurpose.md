## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

**Tweet 1:**
You've got $8,200 at 24.99% APR. That's $171/month in interest alone. A balance transfer card promises to fix this in one move.

It can. Or it can make it way worse.

Here's what 40% of users get wrong (and how to be in the 60% that wins): 🧵

**Tweet 2:**
THE TRAP: You transfer $6,500 to a 0% card. Your old card now shows $0 balance and $7,000 available credit. Your brain screams "free money."

Six months later? You've recharged it to $3,800.

Now you're carrying $6,500 + $3,800. You haven't solved debt. You've expanded it.

**Tweet 3:**
THE FIX: Call your old card issuer immediately after transferring and request they lower your credit limit to $500.

Make new charges physically difficult. Discipline is nice. Impossibility is better.

**Tweet 4:**
TRAP #2: You miss one payment. Any card issuer can void your 0% APR and apply their standard 18-29% rate retroactively.

One forgotten autopay = your entire promo period blown.

**Tweet 5:**
THE FIX: Set automatic minimum payments for the day after your paycheck hits. Then manually pay the rest.

You'll never miss a $25 minimum. You might miss a $400 "planned" payment.

**Tweet 6:**
TRAP #3: The fee math. 

A 4% fee on $10,000 = $500 upfront.

If you save $1,800 in interest, great—net +$1,300.

But if you'd only pay $60 in interest anyway? That $500 fee kills the deal.

Do the math before you apply.

**Tweet 7:**
TRAP #4: "21 months" doesn't mean what you think.

That's 21 billing cycles. Your payment takes 3-5 days to post. If it arrives after the promo ends, you're suddenly charged interest on what remains—sometimes retroactively to month 1.

Plan to pay off in 19 months instead.

**Tweet 8:**
THE WINNING MOVE:

✓ Choose the longest 0% period + lowest fee
✓ Set automatic minimum payments
✓ Kill your old card's credit limit
✓ Pay it off in 19 months (not 21)
✓ Do the fee math first

Balance transfers aren't magic. They're a tactical tool for tactical people.

**Tweet 9:**
The difference between saving $2,200 and going $3,800 deeper into debt comes down to execution, not the card itself.

Which person will you be?
