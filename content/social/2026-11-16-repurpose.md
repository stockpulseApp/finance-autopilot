This is exactly when dollar-cost averaging (DCA) earns its keep.

Month 1: Price $100 → buy 4 shares
Month 2: Price $90 → buy 4.44 shares  
Month 3: Price $80 → buy 5 shares

You've lowered your average cost and accumulated more shares on the dip.

Scale this over 30 years = thousands in extra gains.

4/ DCA works because it mechanizes contrarianism. You buy MORE when prices are low, LESS when prices are high.

No timing. No emotion. Just automatic purchases regardless of headlines.

5/ The uncomfortable truth: Lump-sum investing beats DCA about 68% of the time historically.

But if a 15% drop would make you sell everything and swear off stocks forever? DCA is the smarter play.

You'll capture 85% of gains instead of 0% from panic-selling.

6/ When DCA makes the most sense:

✓ You're psychologically allergic to market timing
✓ You're building positions from paychecks (not a lump sum)
✓ Markets feel genuinely unstable

7/ Implementation is painfully simple:

1. Pick your amount ($100-$1,500/month)
2. Automate the transfer
3. Choose low-cost index funds
4. Never check if the market is scary

Automation removes the friction that kills investors.

8/ Pro move: Increase contributions when markets DROP.

If you have emergency funds sitting in savings and the market falls 12%? Consider boosting your monthly purchase temporarily.

You're buying the discount.

9/ The psychological advantage is worth the mathematical trade-off:

DCA isn't optimal. It's normal.

And normal clients who stay invested beat optimized clients who panic-sell every time.

10/ Stop waiting for the "perfect" entry point.

Start a $500/month investment TODAY.

Your future self won't care if you got the bottom. They'll care that you stayed in the game.

The market rewards patience. Automation makes patience automatic.
