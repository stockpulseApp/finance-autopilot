## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ Stop dividing your old salary by 2,080. That math is killing your freelance income.

When you're salaried at $60K, you're actually worth ~$75K+ as a freelancer once you account for:
- Self-employment taxes (+7.65%)
- Health insurance you now buy
- Business overhead
- Unpaid admin time

Here's the real formula. 🧵

2/ Step 1: Your target annual income ($75K if you made $60K salaried)

Step 2: Add business overhead (~$9K/year for insurance, software, equipment, taxes, health coverage)

Step 3: Calculate realistic billable hours (1,200–1,500/year, not 2,080)

Result: $84K ÷ 1,400 hours = **$60/hour minimum**

3/ You went from $29 to $60 just by doing honest math. That's not greedy—that's breakeven.

4/ The trap: Hourly billing pays you to be slow. Get faster at your craft? You earn *less*.

Better move: Project pricing ($3.5K for website design) + value-based pricing ($6K for copy that adds $30K/month revenue)

5/ Value-based pricing is where real money lives.

A client's $2K website redesign might generate $50K in new sales. You're not billing for your time—you're splitting the upside.

This is how freelancers become business owners.

6/ Quick market reality check before you quote:
- Content writer (beginner): $30–$60/hr
- Experienced writer: $75–$150/hr
- Web developer: $60–$150/hr
- Software dev (senior): $100–$200/hr
- Marketing strategist: $100–$250/hr

Specialize = higher rates.

7/ Pro tip: Know your hourly worth even if you don't bill hourly.

It justifies outsourcing (hire a cleaner at $25/hr? Smart if you bill at $100/hr). It's the anchor for all your pricing models.

8/ One more thing: As a freelancer, you owe quarterly tax payments, not just an annual return. Factor that into your rate—it's a cash flow hit most W-2 workers never experience.

9/ The hardest part of freelancing isn't the work. It's hitting send on that first invoice at a price that actually respects your expertise.

Stop leaving money on the table. Price like a professional.

10/ Your move: Calculate your real minimum rate this week using the formula above. Then never quote below it again.
