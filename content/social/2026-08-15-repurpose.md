1/ Most freelancers leave thousands on the table monthly. Not because their work is bad—because they price like they're asking permission instead of offering value. Let's fix that. 🧵

2/ The trap: You Google "freelance rates," pick something in the middle, and call it a day. But that aggregate data includes beginners, side-giggers, and people in low-cost markets. You're not them.

3/ Here's the real calculation:
• Annual income needed: $65k salary + taxes + insurance + business costs = $91k total
• Billable hours/year: ~1,200 (not 2,080—you have admin, marketing, learning time)
• Minimum rate: $76/hour

4/ Add your experience premium:
• 0-1 year: 1.0×
• 1-3 years: 1.3-1.5×
• 3-5 years specialized: 1.5-2×
• 5+ years niche: 2-3×

That's your real hourly floor.

5/ But don't stop at hourly. The highest earners shift to project-based or value-based pricing. Same work, 30-50% higher revenue.

Example: Bookkeeper at $106/hour charges $1,200/month retainer instead of $850 hourly. Client gets predictable pricing. You get better margins.

6/ Five pricing models (pick what fits your work):
✓ Hourly: Only for undefined scope
✓ Project-based: Flat fee for deliverables
✓ Day-rate: Fixed daily fee
✓ Retainer: Recurring monthly fee (our favorite)
✓ Value-based: Charge the outcome, not the hours

7/ Raising rates without losing clients:
→ Give existing clients 30-60 days notice
→ Grandfather long-term clients at old rates (shows loyalty)
→ Position it as value increase, not greed ("I've added expertise in X")
→ New clients get new rates immediately

8/ The brutal truth: If you lose a client over a rate increase, they were never profitable enough. Better to have 3 premium clients than 10 who pay poverty wages.

Your skills deserve premium pricing. Build your business around that.

9/ Your move: Calculate your real minimum rate this week using the three-step formula. Then audit your current clients. Are you charging what you're worth?

10/ The freelancers earning $100k+ annually didn't work harder—they priced smarter. You can too.
