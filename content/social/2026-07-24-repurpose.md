# X THREAD (10 Tweets Max)

1/ House hacking in 2026: Buy a duplex on an FHA loan (3.5% down), live in one unit, rent the other. Your tenant's rent pays your mortgage. You build equity while your housing cost drops to nearly $0.

2/ Real example: Jordan Chen buys a $285k duplex with $18.5k down. Her mortgage + taxes + insurance = $2,680/month. The other unit rents for $1,350. Her net housing cost: $1,330. She's saving $70/month vs. renting AND building equity.

3/ Year 2 gets spicy: Jordan moves to house hack property #2. Now property #1 generates $2,700/month rent against $2,680 expenses. She's living free while owning 2 properties.

4/ 2026 headwinds are real: Home prices up 38% since 2020. Rates at 6.5-7%. But rental demand is STRONG. Owner-occupant financing still beats investment property loans by miles.

5/ Not all properties work. You need: strong rental demand, decent rent-to-price ratio, neighborhood you'll tolerate living in for 1-3 years, and numbers that actually cash flow.

6/ Property types: Duplexes/triplexes = privacy but harder to find. Single-family + ADU/basement = more inventory but requires conversion costs ($50-150k for ADU buildout).

7/ The math formula: (Total monthly rent) − (mortgage + tax + insurance + PMI + utilities + maintenance reserve) = monthly cash flow. If it's positive, you're winning.

8/ FHA loans are the house hacking weapon: 3.5% down, up to 4 units, must live in one 12 months. On a $300k property, that's $10.5k down vs. $60-75k conventional investment loans require.

9/ What kills most house hacks: skipping the maintenance reserve ($100-150/month). Roofs leak. Water heaters fail. Plan for it.

10/ House hacking won't make you rich overnight in 2026. But it *will* eliminate your biggest expense while building wealth. For first-time buyers? It's one of the highest-leverage moves available.
