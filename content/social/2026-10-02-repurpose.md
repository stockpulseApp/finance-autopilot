## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets Max)

```
1/ Markets tanking? Your finger hovering over "sell all"? This is when dollar-cost averaging (DCA) actually earns its keep.

DCA = investing fixed amounts at regular intervals regardless of price. Boring. Unsexy. Possibly the best circuit breaker between your lizard brain and your retirement account.

2/ The inconvenient truth: lump-sum investing beats DCA 68% of the time because markets trend upward.

But that 32% when DCA wins? Often when it matters most — right before a bear market.

3/ Math example: You inherit $60k. Markets feel scary.

Lump sum today = higher expected returns IF markets keep climbing.

Monthly $5k over 12 months = lower average cost IF markets dip during those 12 months.

You don't know which happens. History says markets rise. Your gut says they'll crash.

4/ Loss aversion is real. Losing $10k feels ~2x worse than gaining $10k feels good.

Lump sum: watch $60k become $48k, panic-sell at bottom.

DCA: next $5k buys MORE shares during the dip. Regret? Diluted.

5/ The real danger: sitting in cash forever waiting for "the right time." Markets hit all-time highs regularly (that's what upward trends DO). Waiting for a crash = waiting forever.

Better to DCA 6 months than perfect-time yourself into never starting.

6/ Already have a 401(k)? You've been dollar-cost averaging the whole time without losing sleep over it.

Autopilot + consistency has minted millions of millionaires who never read a market forecast.

The magic: just keep buying.

7/ Geopolitical chaos, elections, tariffs — none of it matters if your investing plan is locked in.

DCA says: I don't need to predict the next crisis. I just keep buying.

History: markets weathered missiles, 9/11, COVID, and every headline panic.

Consistency beats heroics.

8/ So what's the move?

Pure lump sum = optimal but requires nerves of steel.
Pure 12-month DCA = suboptimal but psychologically livable.

Hybrid: split it. Invest half now, half over 6 months. Best of both worlds.

9/ The point: autopilot beats panic. Boring beats brilliant. A mediocre strategy you actually stick with beats a perfect strategy you abandon when things get scary.

Your retirement account doesn't care how smart you look. It cares that you kept buying.

10/ Dollar-cost averaging isn't about beating the market. It's about beating yourself.
```
