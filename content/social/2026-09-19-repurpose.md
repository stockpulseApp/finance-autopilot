## 1. X THREAD (10 Tweets)

```
1/ Your investment account choice matters MORE than which stocks you pick.

Morgan Stanley data: optimize account structure, earn an extra 1.6% annually over 20 years = 73% more gains.

But most Americans leave this money on the table. Here's the ranking 🧵

2/ #1: Employer 401(k) — Contribute Enough for the Match

Free money. If your employer matches 50¢-$1 per dollar up to 6% of salary, you earn an instant 50-100% return.

Skipping it = leaving a guaranteed raise on the table.

3/ #2: HSA (Health Savings Account)

The triple tax advantage:
✓ Tax-deductible contributions
✓ Tax-free growth
✓ Tax-free withdrawals (for medical expenses)

After 65? Use it like a Roth IRA.

Most efficient account in U.S. tax code.

4/ Pro HSA move: Pay medical bills from checking. Invest the HSA in index funds. Save receipts. Reimburse yourself tax-free 30 years later. This turns medical spending into a hidden Roth.

5/ #3: Roth IRA ($7,000/year)

Max this before extra 401(k) contributions above the match.

$7,000/year from age 32-65 = $913K tax-free at 8% returns.

Same amount taxable? You lose $102K-$136K to capital gains tax.

6/ Roth IRA tip: Hit the income limit? Backdoor Roth converts work (no income cap on conversions). Consult a CPA on pro-rata rules.

7/ #4: 401(k) Above the Match

Higher contribution limit ($23,500 vs. $7,000), but often underperforms Roth IRA on fund choices.

Priority depends on:
• Tax bracket now vs. retirement
• Fund quality in your plan
• Roth 401(k) availability

8/ #5: Traditional IRA — Only if you lack 401(k) access AND earn below the deduction phase-out.

Otherwise? Contributions won't be deductible. Backdoor Roth is better.

9/ #6-8: SEP-IRA (self-employed), 529 (education), and other accounts are lower priority for most—but critical if your situation matches.

10/ The math is simple: max the match → HSA → Roth → then scale the rest.

Account structure compounds as much as asset allocation. Don't ignore it.
```
