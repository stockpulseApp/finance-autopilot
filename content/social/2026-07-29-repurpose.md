3/ ...until you're 62 and laid off. Or dealing with health problems. Or your 401(k) has $40K and rent is $1,800/month. Suddenly the spreadsheet doesn't matter.

7/ The real question isn't "what age maximizes benefits?" It's: Can you afford to wait? Do you have health risks? Is your spouse younger? Are you still working?

8/ There's no universal "right" answer. The person reading this (engaged with finance) statistically lives longer and should wait. But they also *can* wait. The system punishes those who can't.

9/ If you're married, see a fee-only CFP. If you're single and healthy, the math says delay. If you're struggling financially or have health issues, claim and don't look back.

10/ Social Security timing is one of the few 6-figure decisions most Americans make. Get this wrong and you're leaving money on the table for 25+ years. Get it right and it compounds.
