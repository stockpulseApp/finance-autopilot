# Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ Markets down 8%? Your coworker is panicking? Perfect timing to talk about dollar-cost averaging—the unsexy strategy that's kept millions invested through every crash since the 1970s.

2/ Here's the psychology problem DCA solves: You have $18k saved. Markets are up 22%, then drop 12%. Do you buy now? Wait? Regret forever? DCA removes that paralysis by making the decision automatic.

3/ The math is simple: when your target fund drops 15%, your $500 buys 17.6% MORE shares than last month. Those cheap shares participate in the full recovery. That's how crashes create opportunity.

4/ Real example: Someone invested $100/month into Bitcoin since 2015 ($14k total). Despite 80% drawdowns, they spent only 4 months underwater. Why? Crash periods = way more shares at low prices.

5/ BUT—here's what Wall Street won't tell you: lump-sum investing outperforms DCA about 68% of the time. Markets trend up, so earlier money usually wins. The catch? That other 32% can be BRUTAL.

6/ If you lump-sum $50k right before a 15% crash, you're down $7,500 fast. Most investors panic and stop. DCA over 12 months? Max drawdown is ~$2,600. You stay invested. That discipline compounds.

7/ The real value of DCA: It's not about better returns. It's about returns you can actually *stick with* during volatility. Behavioral wins beat optimal timing 9 times out of 10.

8/ DCA playbook: Have a lump sum? Invest 40% now, divide remaining 60% into 6-8 monthly tranches. Never pause during crashes. Let the algorithm do the heavy lifting, not your emotions.

9/ Already investing from salary? Auto-transfer the day after payday. Never "pause" contributions when markets drop. That's the opposite of DCA and why most people fail.

10/ The hardest part isn't math. It's staying disciplined when chaos feels like a signal to stop. It never is. DCA works precisely because it removes your ability to make panic decisions.
```
