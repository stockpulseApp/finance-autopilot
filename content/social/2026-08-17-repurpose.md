## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ Most budgets die in 3 weeks because they're passive.

Zero-based budgeting flips it: instead of tracking guilt, you decide BEFORE the month where every dollar goes.

Income minus expenses = $0. Every dollar has a job.

The difference between managing $47K like $70K earners vs. staying broke.

2/ Traditional budgeting: "Try to spend less"
Zero-based budgeting: "Tell your money exactly what to do or it vanishes into DoorDash"

One is a suggestion. One is a system.

3/ The real problem isn't math—it's STICKING to it.

The solution: assign every dollar UPFRONT so you spend on purpose, not by accident.

Guilt disappears when you pre-approve the purchase.

4/ Jordan made $4,200/month and couldn't figure out where it went.

She tracked 60 days. Discovered $340/month on food delivery + $180 on clothes she never wore.

Seeing the numbers ended the denial.

5/ The framework (5 steps):
1. Know real take-home income
2. Track for 60-90 days (once)
3. List every category
4. Assign every dollar until $0
5. Adjust monthly, reconcile at month-end

That's it. Boring. Effective.

6/ Counterintuitive truth: zero-based budgeting feels MORE freeing than "spend whatever and track it"

Because you already decided. A $40 concert ticket? Check joy fund. Guilt-free. Done.

Decision fatigue kills budgets. Zero-based frontloads decisions to ONE session/month.

7/ If expenses exceed income, you cut or hustle for side income.

No magic. Just math.

Jordan's car needed repairs? She pulled $100 from dining, $60 from clothes, $50 from spontaneous joy. Still zero-based—just reallocated.

8/ Tool recommendation: YNAB for complexity, Sheets for simplicity, EveryDollar for middle ground.

Real answer: the tool matters less than the habit. Find your fit.

9/ Biggest mistake: budgeting for fantasy life.

You're not going to meal-prep every meal if you never have before.

Budget for WHO YOU ARE, not who you think you should be.

10/ Start this month. Track 60 days. Build ONE budget. Stick to it for 90 days.

Then decide if you're the person who finally stops wondering where the money went.
```
