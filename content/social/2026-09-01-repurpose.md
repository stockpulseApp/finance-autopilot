Your priority order should be:
1. HSA (if eligible)
2. Roth IRA
3. 401(k) to match
4. Max out remaining 401(k)
5. Then taxable

Here's why the math matters.

2/ HSAs are broken. Not because they're bad—because they're *insanely* good and everyone sleeps on them.

Triple tax advantage: deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical.

$4,300/year from age 30-65 at 8% returns = $1.05M tax-free.

A taxable account? $840K.

$210K difference. For the same dollars.

3/ The Roth IRA is your second priority if you qualify (income limits apply).

$7K/year from age 25-65 at 8% = $2.04M tax-free.

Same investment, taxable account = $1.68M.

$360K penalty for skipping the Roth.

Decades of compounding >> everything else.

4/ The 401(k) gets tier two status—not because it's weak, but because contribution limits are high enough that most people never max them after HSA + Roth.

**But never skip the match.** 6% employer match = instant 100% return.

$100K salary, 6% match, 30 years = +$679K free.

5/ Income limits block some from Roth IRAs? Use the backdoor Roth.

Contribute to non-deductible traditional IRA → immediately convert to Roth.

Legal. Powerful. Requires a CPA if you have existing traditional IRA balances (pro-rata rule).

6/ Self-employed? A solo 401(k) dwarfs standard IRA limits.

Contribute as both employee ($23.5K) and employer (25% of net self-employment income).

Total: ~$70K/year.

One year's contribution at 8% for 20 years = $214K.

Repeat annually = millions.

7/ The mega backdoor Roth isn't for everyone—only plans that allow after-tax contributions + in-service conversions offer it.

But if yours does? Up to $70K additional annual contribution available.

Ask HR. Most plans don't allow it. Yours might be the exception.

8/ Here's the uncomfortable truth: 59% of investors cite diversification as a top wealth factor.

Only 46% prioritize maxing retirement plans.

But tax-advantaged compounding often delivers *significantly more* long-term wealth than taxable returns on the same dollars.

The gap is real.

9/ Your ranking by wealth-building power:
Tier 1: HSA, Roth IRA
Tier 2: 401(k) match + remaining deferrals
Tier 3: SEP-IRA / Solo 401(k) (self-employed)
Tier 4: 529 plans (education)
Tier 5: PPLI (ultra-wealthy only)

Boring beats clever. Compounding beats timing.

10/ Start with HSA. Max it. Invest it (don't spend it). Then Roth IRA. Then 401(k).

The wealthy aren't smarter than you. They're just older.

And they started earlier with tax-advantaged accounts.
