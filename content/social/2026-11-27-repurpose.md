## 1) X Thread (10 Tweets Max)

```
🧵 House hacking is how first-time buyers are actually affording homes in 2025. Here's the play:

1/ You buy a duplex/triplex with just 3.5% down. Live in one unit. Rent the other. Your tenant covers your mortgage. You build equity. Everyone wins.

2/ Real example: Sophie bought a duplex for $340K, put down just $11,900 (3.5% FHA). Rents the other unit for $1,400/month. Her net housing cost? $1,280/month—less than her old apartment rent.

3/ The secret: owner-occupant loans beat investor loans. 3-5% down vs 20-25% down. FHA counts 75% of projected rental income toward YOUR qualifying income. This changes the math entirely.

4/ A Minnesota investor did it on Airbnb, earned $3,200 in month one, sometimes eliminated his mortgage payment entirely. No trust fund. No family money. Just strategy.

5/ Four common plays:
   • Multi-unit (duplex/triplex/fourplex)
   • ADU (backyard cottage/basement apartment)
   • Rent-by-room (housemates)
   • Short-term rental (Airbnb)

6/ The trap: think of this as passive income and you'll fail. You're now a landlord. Screen tenants hard. Budget for maintenance. Plan for 1-2 months vacancy/year. This isn't free money—it's leverage.

7/ Hot market? Bet on appreciation but don't expect cash flow day one. Cold market? Cash flow immediately but slower appreciation. Know which game you're playing.

8/ The numbers that matter:
   - FHA: 3.5% down, up to 4 units, mortgage insurance required
   - Conventional: 3-5% down, often cheaper long-term
   - VA: 0% down, no mortgage insurance (veterans only)

9/ Exit strategy matters. You must live there 12 months minimum. After that, you can convert to pure rental (refinance as investment property—costs 2-4% in fees) or sell and house hack again.

10/ House hacking isn't a loophole. It's the best leverage available to first-time buyers. Your tenant doesn't just pay rent—they're buying equity in your future.
```
