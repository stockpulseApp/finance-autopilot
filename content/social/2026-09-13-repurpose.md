# Repurposed Content

## X Thread (10 tweets)

1/ Most budgets die in 30 days. Not because you're bad with money—because tracking every latte feels like a second job. Zero-based budgeting fixes this. You assign every dollar a *job before* the month starts. Income minus outgo = zero. Here's how to actually stick to it. 🧵

2/ Step 1: Calculate your REAL take-home pay. Not your gross salary—the money that actually lands in your account. Include side income. If it varies, use your lowest recent month. Example: $7,600 salary + $600 Etsy side gig = $8,200 starting point (minus taxes).

3/ Step 2: List your fixed expenses. Rent, utilities, insurance, debt payments—the stuff you can't skip. Pull 3 months of bank statements. Find those forgotten subscriptions. Hint: they're usually hiding in your autopay. These are your non-negotiables.

4/ Step 3: Build variable categories. Groceries, gas, restaurants, personal care. Be honest about your actual spending, not fantasy spending. Separate essentials from discretionary so you know where to cut if income dips. Review 2-3 months of real spending.

5/ Step 4: Prioritize savings and debt payoff. Savings isn't what's left over—it's a budget category with a job. Emergency fund first, then high-interest debt, then retirement matching, then goals. Every dollar assigned = no money sitting in limbo feeling guilty.

6/ Step 5: Do the math. Income = Fixed + Variable + Savings + Everything Else. If you're short, you need more income (side hustle) or deeper cuts. If you're over, that's your buffer for life's messiness. Every. Dollar. Has. A. Job.

7/ Step 6: Track weekly (15 minutes, not hourly). Use whatever tool won't annoy you into quitting—spreadsheet, YNAB, pen & paper. Mid-month reality check: spent too much groceries? Rebalance from fun money. No guilt, just adjustments.

8/ Why they fail: Too many categories (groceries + toiletries + pet food = analysis paralysis). Started in December (worst month ever). Budgets too tight with zero flexibility. Solution? Keep it simple. Start boring. Build the habit first.

9/ The real secret: "The best budgeting method is the one you'll actually stick to long-term." Zero-based only works if you use it. If simpler works better for you, do that instead. Consistency beats perfection every time.

10/ Ready to build? Start with one month of boring, predictable spending. Assign every dollar. Adjust weekly. By week 4, you'll know if zero-based budgeting is your system or if you need something lighter. Either way, you're ahead of 90% of people with no budget at all.
