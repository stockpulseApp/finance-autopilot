## X Thread (10 tweets max)

1/ Balance transfer cards promise to save you thousands in interest. They can—or they can trap you worse than before. Here's what separates the smart moves from the expensive mistakes. 🧵

2/ The appeal: $8,000 at 24% APR takes 13 years and $9,000 in interest to pay off. A 0% balance transfer card could save you $2,800+ and cut payoff time to under 2 years. But there's fine print.

3/ TRAP #1: The Revert Rate Time Bomb
After your 0% period ends (usually 12-21 months), remaining balances get hit with a revert rate—often 24.99%+. Miss the payoff deadline by even one month and you're paying compound interest on what's left.

4/ The fix: Divide your total balance (including transfer fee) by months of 0% APR. That's your monthly payment to avoid the trap. Pay $400/month on a $12k transfer? You'll owe $4.7k when the promo ends. Math doesn't lie.

5/ TRAP #2: New Purchases Aren't 0%
Buy anything on the balance transfer card, and it's charged at the regular APR (18-25%+) immediately. Your payments prioritize the 0% balance, letting new charges pile up interest. Simple rule: Don't use this card for new purchases.

6/ TRAP #3: One Late Payment Kills Everything
Missing a single payment can cancel your 0% APR and trigger a penalty rate—sometimes higher than your original card. Set up automatic payments. No exceptions.

7/ TRAP #4: Credit Score Requirements
Balance transfer cards need ~690+ credit scores. If your score has dropped from carrying balances, you might not qualify for the best terms. Apply strategically.

8/ When balance transfers WORK:
✅ You have a realistic payoff timeline
✅ You've actually changed your spending habits
✅ You qualify for 15+ months 0% APR
✅ Total savings exceed the transfer fee

9/ The math that matters: Compare your current interest trajectory vs. the transfer scenario (including all fees). Use a balance transfer calculator. Don't just "feel" like it's better.

10/ Balance transfers are tools, not miracles. Used right, they give you breathing room to fix what's broken. Used recklessly, they're financial quicksand. The key question: Are you committed to getting free or just wanting to feel better today?
