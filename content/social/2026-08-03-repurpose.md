# Balance Transfer Cards: Repurposed Content

## 1) X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ Balance transfer cards can save you $1,000+ in interest or silently cost you more. The difference? Execution. Here are the 5 traps that kill most plans.

2/ The math is real: $8k at 22% APR costs $1,760/year in interest. A 0% promo card + aggressive payoff saves ~$1,360 net. But 3 of 5 users never pay off during the window.

3/ TRAP #1: Treating 0% APR like free money. New purchases hit 22%+ immediately while payments chip away at your 0% balance. Lock the card in a drawer. Don't touch it.

4/ TRAP #2: The revert rate shock. After 18-21 months, rates jump to 20%+. If you have a $3k stub left at 24% APR, you're back to $720/year in interest. Build in a 2-month buffer.

5/ TRAP #3: The daisy-chain fantasy. "Transfer to Card A, then Card B for 42 months." Reality: Each transfer is a 3-5% fee + hard inquiry + lower approval odds on the second one.

6/ TRAP #4: Underestimating transfer limits. You don't get to move your entire debt. Maxed utilization tanks your score 20-40 points. Apply strategically when your score is 690+.

7/ TRAP #5: Missing one payment. Many issuers apply penalty APR retroactively to your ENTIRE balance. Your 0% becomes 29.99% overnight. Autopay is non-negotiable.

8/ The win condition: Calculate your breakeven before applying. Run the numbers on NerdWallet or Bankrate. If savings don't justify it, stop. Don't chase a promo for status.

9/ Monthly payment target: Divide balance + fee by months in promo window, then subtract 2 months for margin. Lock it in as autopay. That's it. Discipline over optimization.

10/ One more thing: If you genuinely can't pay it off in 18 months, the problem isn't the card—it's debt-to-income ratio. Talk to a nonprofit credit counselor at NFCC.org instead.
```
