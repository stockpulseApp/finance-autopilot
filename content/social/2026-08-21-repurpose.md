## 1. X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ Balance transfer cards promise salvation from high-interest debt—but they're financial minefields if you don't know the traps. Let's break down when they work and when they wreck you. 🧵

2/ The math sounds great: move $6,000 from 24% APR to 0% for 21 months. But that 3-5% transfer fee? On $6K, that's $300 added to your balance immediately. Your "zero" isn't actually zero.

3/ The biggest trap: treating 0% APR like debt disappeared. It didn't. You still owe every dollar—it's just interest-free for now. If you only pay minimums, most of that balance survives the intro period and gets hit with a 20%+ revert rate.

4/ New purchases? Danger zone. Most balance transfer cards don't offer 0% on new charges—only transfers. That $300 grocery run starts accruing interest immediately while your payments chip away at the old debt first.

5/ The late payment clause is ruthless: miss one due date by even one day and the card issuer can void your 0% APR entirely, jumping you straight to 29.99%+ penalty rates. Flawless payment history is non-negotiable.

6/ Balance transfers only work when THREE things align: (1) you have a real payoff plan, (2) you can stop adding new debt, (3) the math saves more than it costs. If any are missing, skip it.

7/ Rachel paid off $8,200 across 3 cards by committing to $470/month for 18 months. She automated payments and didn't add new charges. The strategy worked because she had a plan—not just a low rate.

8/ Red flags to skip the transfer: carrying $30K+ in debt (transfers won't dent it), credit score below 670, or underlying spending problems. Fix the root cause first, not just the symptoms.

9/ The real question isn't "Do I qualify?" It's "Can I realistically pay this off before the intro period ends?" If the answer is no, you're just kicking the can down the road with a higher interest rate waiting on the other side.

10/ Balance transfers are tools, not solutions. Use them strategically to save thousands—or ignore these traps and dig yourself deeper. Choose wisely.
```
