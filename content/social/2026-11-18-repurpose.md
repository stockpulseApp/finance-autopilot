The concept: Buy a property, live in part of it, rent out the rest. The rental income slashes your housing costs—sometimes to zero. You're still building equity, but now someone else's money is helping foot the bill.

Why lenders love house hackers:
• Lower down payments (3.5% FHA vs 20%+ for investment)
• Better interest rates (0.5-1% lower)
• Easier qualification (lenders count 75% of projected rent income)

**Tweet 5:**
5 house hacking strategies ranked by budget:

1️⃣ Duplex/triplex: $12K-$25K to start (highest privacy)
2️⃣ Single-family + roommates: $9K-$18K (lowest privacy)
3️⃣ ADU/garage conversion: $40K-$150K (long-term passive income)
4️⃣ Short-term rental: High work, high reward
5️⃣ Live-in flip: Reno skills required

**Tweet 6:**
The duplex play is the gold standard. Separate units = separate entrances = fewer awkward hallway encounters with your tenant. You're building equity in a multi-unit asset while maintaining sanity.

**Tweet 7:**
Room rental strategy: A bedroom that leases for $800/month might gross $2,400+ on Airbnb if booked 20 nights at $120/night. But check local short-term rental laws first—fines can hit thousands.

**Tweet 8:**
Hidden costs nobody warns about:
• Maintenance: Budget 1-2% of property value annually
• Vacancy cushion: Can you cover mortgage for 2-3 months solo?
• Your time: Expect 3-6 hours/month managing the property

**Tweet 9:**
One year rule: Buy as owner-occupied with favorable financing, then move out after 12 months and rent your unit too. You keep the low rates and terms. Property becomes a full rental.
