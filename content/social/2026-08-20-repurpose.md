## 1. X Thread (10 tweets max)

```
1/ Your rent just hit $2,100/month. You're 28, making $72K, and half your paycheck vanishes into housing. There's a better way: house hacking. Here's how Mia Chen escaped the rent trap and started building wealth instead. 🧵

2/ House hacking = buy a duplex/triplex, live in one unit, rent the others. Your tenant's rent covers most (or all) of your mortgage. Mia bought a $385K Indianapolis duplex. Her tenant pays $1,400. She lives for $1,250/month instead of $2,100. The math works.

3/ The secret sauce? Owner-occupant financing. Because you live there, lenders unlock:
• FHA loans at 3.5% down (not 20%)
• Better interest rates (0.5-0.75% lower)
• Access to 2-4 unit properties as "primary residence"
On a $400K property, that's $80K-$100K saved upfront.

4/ But there's a catch: you *must* occupy the property for 12 months. After that, you're free to move out, convert it fully to rentals, or house-hack property #2. One-year occupancy lock isn't a bug—it's a feature protecting lenders (and you).

5/ The numbers only work in specific markets. Indianapolis, Cleveland, Kansas City, Memphis, Oklahoma City—Midwest/Sun Belt cities where rent-to-price ratios hit sweet spots. In San Francisco or Boston? A $900K duplex won't pencil; you'll be subsidizing forever.

6/ Real costs people ignore:
• Vacancy (5-10% of annual rent)
• Maintenance (1% of property value/year)
• Property management if you outsource (8-12% of rent)
• Insurance, HOA, utilities
• Legal compliance & screening
Budget $333-500/month *before* tenant drama.

7/ The non-financial truth: house hacking trades *privacy for equity*. Your tenant is your neighbor. Bad tenant selection = living next to chaos. You're on-call for repairs. Your friends rent chic lofts. You mow a duplex lawn. The payoff is years away.

8/ Where it goes sideways: underestimating repairs, market timing risk, overleveraging into multiple properties, regulatory changes, and personality mismatch. If you hate conflict or value solitude, on-site landlording will destroy you.

9/ Mia's year-one P&L:
• Mortgage + taxes + insurance: $2,700/month
• Rental income: $1,400
• Net housing cost: $1,300 (vs. her old $2,100 rent)
• She saves $480/month while building equity in a $385K asset
• Year 2: move out, rent both units, generate positive cash flow

10/ House hacking isn't for everyone. But if you're willing to sacrifice privacy for 12 months and have the discipline to treat it like a business, it's the most powerful wealth-building shortcut available to first-time buyers. The catch? You have to actually do it.
```
